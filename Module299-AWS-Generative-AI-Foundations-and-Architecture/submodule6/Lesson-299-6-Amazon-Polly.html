Amazon Polly

How Amazon Polly turns text into speech: the four voice engines, SSML, lexicons and speech marks, real-time, long audio, and streaming synthesis, and the architecture, security, and cost of a speech pipeline.

Lesson Overview

Amazon Polly is a managed text-to-speech (TTS) service. You send it text, and it returns lifelike speech, so an application can talk. You call it through an application programming interface (API), and you need no machine learning (ML) expertise. This lesson follows FlashCart Inc., a fictional online marketplace that wants to read articles aloud, voice its phone menu, and add spoken help to its mobile app. In this lesson, you will study:

  • Introduction to Amazon Polly: what the service does, how you call it, and common use cases.
  • Voices and Engines: the standard, neural, long-form, and generative engines, and how to choose a voice.
  • Controlling Speech: Speech Synthesis Markup Language (SSML), pronunciation lexicons, and speech marks.
  • Real-Time, Long Audio, and Streaming: the three ways to synthesize speech, with their limits.
  • Architecture, Security, and Cost: caching, pipelines, throttling, data privacy, and the pricing model.
  • Comparison and Scenarios: how Polly compares with other AWS artificial intelligence (AI) services, and an SAA-C03 cheat sheet.
  • Knowledge Check: open-ended questions with model answers.

Learning Objectives

At the end of this lesson, learners will be able to:

  1. Define Amazon Polly and TTS, and state where Polly fits in the AWS AI layers.
  2. Describe the four voice engines, and the features, Regions, and limits that differ between them.

  5. Use pronunciation lexicons and speech marks, and explain how they differ.

  7. Design a speech pipeline that caches audio and works with Amazon Lex, Amazon Connect, Amazon Transcribe, or Amazon Translate.

1. Introduction to Amazon Polly

Problem Statement

FlashCart Inc. publishes a daily buying guide, runs a customer phone line, and has a mobile app for shoppers with low vision. Today the guide is text only, the phone menu uses a recording that a voice actor made two years ago, and every menu change costs a new studio session.

The engineering manager wants spoken output that the team can change in minutes. The team has no speech scientists. She asks what Polly can do, what formats the audio comes in, and whether FlashCart may store and reuse the audio that Polly creates.

She also wants to know whether the service can read the same text in several languages.

Your challenge: Advise the manager. Your answer should:

  • Explain what Polly does and why it needs no ML expertise.
  • Name the audio formats and say what FlashCart can do with the audio.
  • Say how Polly handles several languages and voices.

1.1 What Amazon Polly Is

  • Amazon Polly is a service that turns text into lifelike speech. You send the text to the Polly API, and Polly returns the audio at once as a stream that you can play, or save as a standard audio file such as MP3.
  • It uses deep learning, and it lets existing applications speak as a first-class feature, in cars, mobile apps, devices, and appliances.
  • It includes many lifelike voices in many languages, so that you can choose the voice and the language for your listeners.
  • It supports Speech Synthesis Markup Language (SSML) tags, such as prosody, so that you can change the speaking rate, pitch, or volume.
  • It sits in the AI services layer, with Amazon Rekognition, Amazon Textract, Amazon Comprehend, Amazon Translate, and Amazon Transcribe. Polly goes from text to speech, and Transcribe goes the other way, from speech to text.

1.2 From Text to Audio

1.3 How You Call Polly

  • You send text, either plain text or SSML, and you choose a voice. You can also choose the engine, the output format, and the sampling rate.
  • Polly supports MP3, Ogg Vorbis, and raw PCM audio stream formats. You can stream the audio to your users in near real time.
  • Polly works with all AWS SDK languages, such as Java, Node.js, .NET, PHP, Python, Ruby, Go, and C++, with the AWS mobile SDKs, and with an HTTP API.
  • To try it, open the Polly console, type text, and listen to the result or save it as an audio file.
  • If you do not name an engine, Polly uses the standard engine, and a voice that the standard engine does not support causes an error.

1.4 Languages and Voices

  • Polly includes dozens of voices in many languages. The supported languages page lists them, and the available voices page shows which engines each voice supports.
  • A voice can be bilingual. For example, the voice Aditi can speak Indian English or Hindi. If you do not give a language code, Polly uses the default language of the voice.
  • To read one text in several languages, translate the text first with Amazon Translate, and then synthesize each version with a voice for its language.

1.6 Reusing the Audio

  • You can cache and replay the speech that Polly generates at no additional cost, and there are no restrictions on storing and reusing it.
  • You own the audio that Polly creates from your text. If you send text that belongs to someone else, you need the rights to use it.
  • For fixed prompts, such as a phone menu, create the audio once and store it, instead of synthesizing it for every call.

2.2 Neural Voices and the Newscaster Style

  • The neural engine can produce higher-quality voices than the standard engine, and it is available in a subset of the Regions.
  • The Newscaster speaking style makes a voice sound like a TV or radio newscaster. It works only with the neural engine, for the US English voices Matthew and Joanna, the British English voice Amy, and the US Spanish voice Lupe.
  • That is why a newscaster demo works with one voice and fails with another: the style is limited to select neural voices.

2.3 Long-Form Voices

  • The long-form engine is built for content that holds a listener’s attention for a long time, such as news articles, training materials, and marketing videos.
  • It offers a small set of voices, in US English and Spain Spanish, and it is available only in the US East (N. Virginia) Region.
  • It supports real-time and asynchronous synthesis, and all speech marks.

2.4 Generative Voices

  • The generative engine is the largest Polly model. It turns text into speech codes with a billion-parameter transformer, and then a decoder makes the audio in a streamable way.
  • It gives emotionally engaged, colloquial speech, for example for a customer assistant, a virtual trainer, or an advertiser.
  • It supports bidirectional streaming, so that text can go in while audio comes out. It does not support the Newscaster style, and speech marks are not available.
  • It is available in a limited set of Regions, and the number of voices keeps growing, so check the generative voices page for the current list.
  • Because updates to the model can change how a voice sounds, content that is synthesized over a long period, such as a season of podcasts, can vary slightly.
  • AWS says that a built-in safety mechanism reduces the risk of hallucinated speech, but it does not remove it. Do not assume complete protection.

2.5 Brand Voice

  • Brand Voice is a custom engagement. You work with the Polly team to build a neural voice that only your organization uses.
  • You start by contacting your AWS account manager. The cost and the timeline depend on your goals.

3. Controlling Speech

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s first audio sounds right, except in places. The product name “FlashCart” is split into two words, the abbreviation “FX-200” is read letter by letter, and a long list of numbers runs together with no pauses. The marketing team also wants a slow, emphasized voice for a sale announcement.

The mobile team wants to highlight each word on screen as it is spoken, and to move the mouth of a cartoon mascot with the speech. The security team asks whether the pronunciation fixes can be stored once and applied to every request.

The developer tried a whispering effect and word emphasis, and Polly returned an error with the neural voice.

Your challenge: Choose the right control for each problem. Your answer should:

  • Name the tool for pauses, speed, and emphasis, and the tool for fixed pronunciations.
  • Name the feature for word highlighting and mouth movement.
  • Explain the error that the developer saw.

3.1 Plain Text and SSML

  • You can send plain text, or text marked up with Speech Synthesis Markup Language (SSML), a standard XML-based markup for speech synthesis. SSML needs <speak> tags around the text, and the request must say that the text type is SSML.
  • With SSML you can add pauses, change the speaking rate, pitch, and volume, specify how numbers and dates are spoken, and give a phonetic pronunciation.
  • The SSML in a request must be valid and well formed. Polly returns an error for invalid SSML.
  • SSML tags are not billed as characters.

  • The standard engine supports all tags except the newscaster tag.
  • If you use an SSML tag that the standard, neural, or long-form engine does not support, you get an error. That is the developer’s result with the whispering effect and emphasis on a neural voice.
  • Other limits: the <audio>, <lexicon>, <lookup>, and <voice> tags are not supported, a single <break> can last up to 10 seconds, and the <prosody> rate cannot go below minus 80 percent.

3.3 Pronunciation Lexicons

  • A pronunciation lexicon lets you change how Polly says particular words, such as company names, acronyms, foreign words, and new words. Examples are a stylized name, such as “P!nk,” an acronym, such as “ROTFL,” and a phrase like “C’est la vie” in a non-French voice.
  • For example, a lexicon can map the acronym AWS to “Amazon Web Services,” or a stylized spelling of a name to the name itself. You can also give the pronunciation with a phonetic alphabet.
  • You store a lexicon with the Polly API, and then you name it in the SynthesizeSpeech request. A lexicon is stored in an AWS Region and is used from that Region.
  • A lexicon is applied only if its language is the same as the language of the voice.
  • Limits: up to 100 lexicons in an account, a name of up to 20 alphanumeric characters, up to 40,000 characters in a lexicon, up to 100 characters in each replacement, and up to five lexicons in one request. A larger lexicon adds latency.

3.4 Speech Marks

  • Speech marks are metadata that Polly returns with the speech, so that your application can do speech-synchronized animation and karaoke-style highlighting.
  • There are four kinds: sentence, word, viseme, and ssml.
  • A word or a sentence mark tells you when it is spoken. A viseme mark describes the shape of the lips for a sound, which you can use to move an avatar’s mouth. An ssml mark follows an SSML element in the text.
  • Speech marks come as a JSON stream: standalone JSON objects, each on its own line. You request them with the SpeechMarkTypes parameter and the JSON output format.
  • Speech marks are not available with generative voices.
  • You are charged for a request for speech and for a request for speech marks. If you request 1,000 characters of speech and the speech marks for the same text, you pay for 2,000 characters.

4.2 Long Audio With a Synthesis Task

  • You call StartSpeechSynthesisTask with the text, the voice, the output format, and the name of an Amazon S3 bucket. You can add an S3 key prefix, and an Amazon SNS topic to receive status notifications.
  • Polly runs the task asynchronously and saves the audio file in your bucket.
  • You can check on a task with GetSpeechSynthesisTask and list tasks with ListSpeechSynthesisTasks. The task object is available for 72 hours.
  • The output format can be MP3, Ogg Vorbis, or PCM, or JSON for speech marks.

4.3 Bidirectional Streaming

  • Bidirectional streaming sends text to Polly and receives audio at the same time over one stream. It suits a conversational assistant that generates its answer in pieces.
  • It works only with generative voices, in a subset of the Regions that support them. It supports SSML and lexicons, but not speech marks.
  • Up to 8 concurrent stream requests are supported, and 8 requests per second can start a stream.

4.4 Audio Formats and Sampling Rates

  • Polly supports MP3, Ogg Vorbis, and raw PCM audio.
  • For MP3 and Ogg Vorbis, the sampling rates are 8, 16, 22.05, and 24 kHz. The default is 22.05 kHz for standard voices and 24 kHz for neural, long-form, and generative voices. For PCM, the rates are 8 and 16 kHz, with 16 kHz as the default.
  • Choose a rate that balances bandwidth and audio quality for your application.
Exam trap

A single SynthesizeSpeech call is limited to 3,000 billed characters and 10 minutes of audio. For a long text, use an asynchronous synthesis task, or split the text. Also remember that streaming input and output together works only with the generative engine, and that a synthesis task writes its audio to Amazon S3, not to the response.

Memory hook

A short answer is a call. A whole chapter is a task. A live conversation is a stream.

5. Architecture, Security, and Cost

Problem Statement

FlashCart wants three things: audio for every new buying guide as soon as it is published, a voice for its phone menu that answers callers, and a way to keep the cost of the same prompts from growing with every call.

A launch campaign pushes the number of simultaneous requests above what Polly allowed, and some requests failed with a throttling error. The security team asks who can use Polly and what AWS does with the text. The finance team asks how Polly is billed.

The architect must draw the designs and answer both teams.

Your challenge: Design the solution. Your answer should:

  • Show how a new article becomes an audio file, and how a phone call is answered.
  • Explain how to avoid throttling and how to keep cost down.
  • Describe access control, privacy, and billing.

5.1 A Pipeline for New Articles

  1. A new article is saved to an Amazon S3 bucket.
  2. The upload event starts an AWS Lambda function, which starts a Polly synthesis task with an output bucket and an SNS topic.
  3. Polly saves the audio file in S3 and publishes the status to the SNS topic.
  4. The website serves the audio file through Amazon CloudFront, so that listeners get it fast.

The same design can feed audio to a mobile app, or add a spoken version to a newsletter.

5.2 Voice Menus and Voice Assistants

  • With Amazon Lex, you can build voice user interfaces. With Amazon Connect, Polly speech is used for cloud-based self-service contact centers.
  • Polly can also voice IVR systems, announcement systems, and IoT devices.
  • Polly can chain with other services: Amazon Transcribe, Amazon Translate, and Polly together let a person speak in one language and hear another (Lesson 299.5).

5.3 Throttling and Scaling

OperationDefault limit
SynthesizeSpeech with standard voices80 requests per second, with a burst of 100, and up to 80 concurrent requests.
SynthesizeSpeech with neural or long-form voices8 requests per second, with a burst of 10. Neural allows up to 18 concurrent requests, and long-form up to 26.
SynthesizeSpeech with generative voices8 requests per second, and up to 26 concurrent requests.
StartSpeechSynthesisTask10 requests per second for standard and neural voices, and 1 per second for long-form and generative voices.
StartSpeechSynthesisStream8 requests per second, and up to 8 concurrent requests.
  • Excess requests are rejected with an HTTP 400 status and a ThrottlingException header.
  • Retry throttled requests with exponential backoff and jitter, which spreads the load over a short time.
  • Use the Amazon Polly metrics in Amazon CloudWatch to analyze usage and forecast growth.
  • You can ask for an increase of the adjustable quotas in the console. First calculate the requests per second that you need. Playing the audio takes seconds, which lowers the rate of calls.
  • The best way to avoid throttling is to not call Polly at all for repeated content: cache the audio.

5.4 Security and Data Privacy

  • Use AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM) policies and roles to control who can call Polly and who can read the audio in Amazon S3.
  • AWS says that it protects content with encryption at rest and in transit, and that only authorized employees can access it.
  • Polly may store and use the text that it processes to provide the service and, unless you opt out, to improve Polly and other Amazon AI technologies. To opt out, use an AWS Organizations AI services opt-out policy.
  • Content is stored at rest in the Region where you use Polly, and a portion may be stored in another Region for improvement of the service unless you opt out.
  • You own your content and the audio that Polly creates from it.
  • Polly is a HIPAA eligible service under the AWS Business Associate Addendum, and with that in place PHI is handled as the addendum says.

5.5 The Cost Model

  • You pay for the number of characters that you send, and the price depends on the engine. Check the pricing page for current rates and the free tier.
  • SSML tags are not billed as characters.
  • A request for speech marks is billed separately, so speech and speech marks for the same text cost twice the characters.
  • Caching and replaying speech costs nothing extra, so synthesize fixed prompts once and store the audio.
  • Choose the cheapest engine that meets the quality need. Use standard for short prompts, and use the premium engines where their quality matters.
  • Brand Voice is a custom engagement. Its cost depends on the goals, so contact your AWS account manager.
Exam trap

Do not synthesize the same fixed prompt for every call. Caching costs nothing and avoids throttling. Also remember that neural, long-form, and generative engines have much lower default request rates than standard, so a traffic spike hits them first. Polly may store and use your text to improve the service unless you opt out with an AWS Organizations policy.

6.5 Lesson Summary

  • Amazon Polly is a managed text-to-speech service in the AI services layer. You send text and get lifelike audio in MP3, Ogg Vorbis, or PCM, with no ML expertise. You can cache and reuse the audio at no extra cost.
  • Polly has four engines: standard, neural, long-form, and generative. They differ in quality, Regions, speech marks, the Newscaster style, and request rates. A request without an engine uses standard.
  • SSML controls pauses, rate, pitch, volume, and pronunciation, but tag support differs by engine: emphasis, whispering, and breathing sounds work only with standard voices. Lexicons fix pronunciations for all requests, and speech marks give word, sentence, viseme, and SSML timing.
  • SynthesizeSpeech handles up to 3,000 billed characters and 10 minutes of audio. A synthesis task handles up to 100,000 billed characters and saves to Amazon S3. Bidirectional streaming works with generative voices.
  • Polly pairs with Lex and Connect for voice menus, and with Transcribe and Translate for speech in one language and out in another. It may store and use your text to improve the service unless you opt out.

7. Knowledge Check

Answer each question in your own words before you open the model answer.

  1. Explain what Amazon Polly is, and why FlashCart can use it with no ML engineers.

    Show the model answer

    Polly is a managed text-to-speech service. FlashCart sends text to the API and gets lifelike audio back as a stream or a file. AWS runs the deep learning models and the infrastructure, so no ML expertise is needed.

  2. Which audio formats does Polly support, and what can you do with the audio?

    Show the model answer

    Polly supports MP3, Ogg Vorbis, and raw PCM. You can play the audio in near real time, or save it as a file. You can cache and replay the speech at no additional cost, with no restriction on storing and reusing it, and you own the audio.

  3. Name the four Polly engines and how they differ.

    Show the model answer

    Standard concatenates recorded speech and has the widest Region coverage. Neural uses a neural network and a vocoder for more natural speech and supports the Newscaster style on select voices. Long-form is for long narration and is available only in US East (N. Virginia). Generative is the largest model and the most expressive, with streaming input and output, but it has no speech marks.

  4. Why does a newscaster demo work with one voice and fail with another?

    Show the model answer

    The Newscaster style works only with the neural engine, for select voices: the US English voices Matthew and Joanna, the British English voice Amy, and the US Spanish voice Lupe. Other voices and engines do not support it.

  5. What happens if a request names no engine, or an SSML tag that the engine does not support?

    Show the model answer

    A request without an engine uses the standard engine, and a voice that standard does not support causes an error. An SSML tag that the standard, neural, or long-form engine does not support also returns an error. For example, emphasis, whispering, and breathing sounds work only with standard voices.

  6. What can SSML do, and what are some limits?

    Show the model answer

    SSML lets you add pauses, change the speaking rate, pitch, and volume, control how numbers and dates are spoken, and give phonetic pronunciations. Tags are not billed. Limits: the audio, lexicon, lookup, and voice tags are not supported, a break can last up to 10 seconds, and the prosody rate cannot go below minus 80 percent.

  7. What is a pronunciation lexicon, and how do you use it?

    Show the model answer

    A lexicon changes how Polly says particular words, such as company names, acronyms, and foreign words. You store it with the Polly API in a Region, and you name it in the synthesis request, up to five in one request. It applies only if its language matches the voice. An account can have up to 100 lexicons.

  8. What are speech marks, and what are their limits?

    Show the model answer

    Speech marks are metadata that come back as a JSON stream with the speech. The four kinds are sentence, word, viseme, and ssml. They support highlighting and lip sync. They are billed as a separate request, so speech and marks for the same text cost twice the characters, and they are not available with generative voices.

  9. Compare SynthesizeSpeech, a synthesis task, and bidirectional streaming.

    Show the model answer

    SynthesizeSpeech returns audio in the response, for up to 3,000 billed characters and 10 minutes of audio. A synthesis task is asynchronous, takes up to 100,000 billed characters, and saves the audio to Amazon S3, with an optional SNS topic. Bidirectional streaming sends text events and receives audio events at the same time, and it works only with generative voices.

  10. Draw a pipeline that turns each new article into an audio file.

    Show the model answer

    An article is saved to Amazon S3. An S3 event starts a Lambda function, which starts a Polly synthesis task with an output bucket and an SNS topic. Polly saves the audio in S3 and publishes the status. The website serves the audio through Amazon CloudFront.

  11. How do you stop throttling and control cost for repeated prompts?

    Show the model answer

    Synthesize each fixed prompt once, store the audio, and replay it, because caching costs nothing extra. Retry throttled requests with exponential backoff and jitter, watch the Polly metrics in CloudWatch, and ask for a quota increase if needed. Standard allows 80 requests per second, while neural, long-form, and generative allow 8.

  12. What does AWS do with the text that Polly processes, and how can an organization opt out?

    Show the model answer

    Polly may store and use text inputs to provide the service and, unless you opt out, to improve Polly and other Amazon AI technologies. You own your content and the audio. To opt out, use an AWS Organizations AI services opt-out policy. Polly is HIPAA eligible under the AWS Business Associate Addendum.

  13. How is Polly billed?

    Show the model answer

    You pay for the characters that you send, and the price depends on the engine. SSML tags are not billed. Speech marks are billed as a separate request. Caching and replaying audio is free, and Brand Voice is a custom engagement priced by scope.

  14. How does Polly work with Lex, Connect, Transcribe, and Translate?

    Show the model answer

    Lex builds the voice interface, and Connect uses Polly speech for self-service contact centers. Transcribe, Translate, and Polly together turn speech in one language into speech in another: Transcribe makes text, Translate converts it, and Polly speaks it.
