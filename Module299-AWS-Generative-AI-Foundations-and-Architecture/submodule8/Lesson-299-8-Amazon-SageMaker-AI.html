Amazon SageMaker AI

How Amazon SageMaker AI builds, trains, and deploys custom machine learning models: environments, training jobs, inference options, pipelines, the model registry and feature store, and the architecture and cost of a prediction system.

Lesson Overview

Amazon SageMaker AI is a fully managed service for developers and data scientists to build, train, and deploy machine learning (ML) models at scale. The other services in this module, such as Amazon Rekognition and Amazon Comprehend, are pre-trained. SageMaker AI is what you use when you need a model of your own. This lesson follows FlashCart Inc., a fictional online marketplace that wants to predict which customers are about to leave. In this lesson, you will study:

  • Introduction to Amazon SageMaker AI: what the service does, the ML workflow, and when to choose it over a pre-trained AI service.
  • Building and Training Models: the environments, training jobs, built-in algorithms, automatic model tuning, and ways to cut training cost.
  • Deploying Models and Getting Predictions: real-time, serverless, asynchronous, and batch transform inference.
  • Automating and Governing ML: pipelines, the model registry, the feature store, and experiments.
  • Architecture and Cost: a churn prediction design and the cost model.
  • Comparison and Scenarios: how SageMaker AI compares with the AI services, and an SAA-C03 cheat sheet.
  • Knowledge Check: open-ended questions with model answers.

1. Introduction to Amazon SageMaker AI

Problem Statement

FlashCart Inc. loses customers, and the marketing team learns about it only after they are gone. The data team has three years of customer history in Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3): orders, returns, support contacts, and whether each customer later left. The team wants a model that gives each customer a churn score, so that marketing can act early.

The engineering manager points out that none of the pre-trained services in this module does this. Rekognition reads images, Textract reads documents, and Comprehend reads text. She asks what to use, and she worries about the work of setting up servers, training the model, and putting it into production.

She also asks which people on the team can use it, because only one of them writes code.

Your challenge: Advise the manager. Your answer should:

  • Explain what SageMaker AI is and when to choose it over a pre-trained service.
  • Describe the steps from data to predictions.
  • Say how the team members who do not code can still take part.

1.1 What Amazon SageMaker AI Is

  • Amazon SageMaker AI is a fully managed service that lets developers and data scientists build, train, and deploy ML models at scale.
  • Doing all the ML steps in one place, with the servers, is usually hard. SageMaker AI brings the steps together and provisions and manages the infrastructure for you.
  • It is for custom models. The AI services in this module, such as Rekognition, Textract, Comprehend, Translate, Transcribe, Polly, and Lex, are pre-trained for one task each, and you call them with an API. With SageMaker AI, you train a model on your own data for your own task.

1.2 The Machine Learning Process

In supervised learning, you train a model on examples that have the right answer, called labels, and then you ask the model for predictions on new data. The slide below uses a simple example: predicting an exam score.

  • Historical data: the examples, with the facts about each one, called features, such as years of experience.
  • Labels: the right answer for each example, such as the score.
  • Train and tune: the algorithm learns from the labeled data, and you adjust its settings, called hyperparameters, to improve it.
  • Apply the model: you give it new data, and it returns a prediction. Using a trained model to predict is called inference.

1.3 The Full Workflow in SageMaker AI

  1. Prepare labeled data and store it in Amazon S3.
  2. Train a model on the labeled data.
  3. Test the trained model on data that it has not seen, and read the predictions with their confidence.
  4. Tune. If the results are not good enough, change the features or the hyperparameters, add more data, and train again.
  5. Deploy the model to an endpoint.
  6. Request inferences. Applications send requests and get predictions.

1.5 Who Can Use It

  • Developers and data scientists use Studio, notebooks, and the AWS software development kit (SDK) to build and train models with code.
  • Business analysts can use SageMaker Canvas, a no-code service that builds models and makes predictions with no coding.
  • People with no ML knowledge can use SageMaker Autopilot, which builds classification and regression models quickly.

2.2 What a Training Job Does

  • You store the training data in Amazon S3.
  • You choose the algorithm as a container image, from Amazon Elastic Container Registry (Amazon ECR), and the instances to train on.
  • SageMaker AI provisions the instances, runs the training job, and saves the resulting model artifact in Amazon S3. You pay for the training time, and the instances go away when the job ends.
  • You can start a training job from the console, from the AWS SDK, or from a notebook.

2.3 Three Ways to Choose the Algorithm

ApproachWhat you provideBest for
Built-in algorithmsYour data. AWS provides the algorithm in a container.Fast experiments with standard algorithms, such as XGBoost, Linear Learner, BlazingText, Image Classification, and Object Detection.
Script modeYour own training script. SageMaker AI provides a framework container, such as TensorFlow, PyTorch, or MXNet.Custom code in a popular framework.
Bring your own containerYour own container image in Amazon ECR.Full control of the environment.

Each built-in algorithm expects its data in particular formats. For example, XGBoost takes comma-separated values (CSV), and Linear Learner takes the RecordIO-protobuf format.

2.4 Tuning, Tracking, and Scale

  • Automatic model tuning, also called hyperparameter tuning, runs many training jobs with different hyperparameter values, within ranges that you set, and finds the best configuration. It replaces tuning by hand.
  • SageMaker Experiments tracks and manages your experiments, so that you can reproduce a run, build on a colleague’s work, and trace model lineage for audits.
  • Distributed training spreads a training job over several instances or several GPUs, so that large jobs finish sooner.
  • SageMaker HyperPod provides an always-on ML environment on resilient clusters for developing very large models, such as large language models (LLMs).
  • SageMaker training plans reserve compute capacity for large training workloads, with a specified timeline and a predictable cost.

2.5 Managed Spot Training

  • Managed Spot Training runs your training on spare Amazon EC2 capacity, and it can lower the cost of training by up to 90 percent compared with on-demand instances.
  • Spot capacity can be taken back, so a job can be interrupted. SageMaker AI manages the interruptions for you.
  • Use checkpoints, so that a job resumes from the last checkpoint instead of starting over. SageMaker AI supports checkpointing for AWS Deep Learning Containers and for some built-in algorithms, including Image Classification, Object Detection, Semantic Segmentation, and XGBoost. For custom scripts, you save and load checkpoints yourself.
  • It fits training that can wait. Automatic model tuning can also use Spot training.
Exam trap

A training job reads from and writes to Amazon S3, and its instances exist only while it runs: do not leave training running on idle instances. Also, Spot training can save up to 90 percent, but it can be interrupted, so use checkpoints for long jobs. And automatic model tuning runs many training jobs, so it multiplies the cost of one job.

3. Deploying Models and Getting Predictions

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s churn model is trained. The website needs a score for the customer who is on the page right now, within a fraction of a second, all day. Marketing also wants every customer scored once each night, and the data team has another model that analyzes a 600 MB log file for each seller, which takes about 20 minutes per file.

The data scientist deployed the first model on a large always-on endpoint and noticed that it costs money all night, when hardly anyone visits. Another developer needs a model that is called only a few times a day by an internal tool.

The architect must match each prediction need with the right deployment option.

Your challenge: Choose the inference option. Your answer should:

  • Name the option for the live website score, the nightly scoring, and the 20-minute log analysis.
  • Name the option for the internal tool that is called a few times a day.
  • Say how to avoid paying for idle capacity.

3.2 More About Real-Time Endpoints

  • A real-time endpoint is a persistent REST API. You call it with the InvokeEndpoint operation, through an AWS SDK or the web API directly.
  • It uses auto scaling to match the invocation volume.
  • A multi-model endpoint hosts many models behind one endpoint, which is a real-time option that lets the models share the instances.
  • You can bring a model that you trained outside SageMaker AI and deploy it on any of the hosting options.
  • Deployment guardrails, with blue/green or rolling deployments, release a new model version in a controlled way. Shadow tests compare a change to your model-serving setup with what is deployed now before you switch.

3.3 Choosing the Right Size

  • SageMaker Inference Recommender gives recommendations on the instance types and configurations to use for your model, so that you do not have to run benchmarks by hand.
  • Picking the right hosting option is the main cost lever for inference. An always-on endpoint costs money even when nobody calls it.

3.4 Which Option for Which Need?

NeedUse

Exam trap

Do not use a real-time endpoint for work that is occasional or offline: you pay for its instances while it is up. Use serverless inference for occasional calls, batch transform for scoring a dataset, and asynchronous inference for large payloads and long processing. Also remember that serverless inference is limited to small payloads and 60 seconds, so large inputs need an asynchronous endpoint.

Memory hook

Real-time is a shop that is always open. Serverless opens when a customer comes. Async takes orders in a queue. Batch does the whole stack overnight.

4. Automating and Governing ML

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s churn model works, but every retraining is a manual series of steps that only the data scientist remembers. Last month an older model went live by mistake, and nobody could say which data it was trained on.

The data team also found that the features used in training were computed differently in the website code, so the live scores were off. The compliance team wants every model documented and approved before it is deployed.

The architect must propose a way to automate the steps and to keep control of the models.

Your challenge: Propose the process. Your answer should:

  • Name the feature that runs the steps in order.
  • Name the feature that versions and approves models.
  • Name the feature that gives training and inference the same features.

4.1 SageMaker Pipelines

  • SageMaker Model Building Pipelines lets you create and manage ML pipelines that are integrated directly with SageMaker AI jobs.
  • A pipeline defines the ML workflow as code. Typical steps are processing the data, training, evaluating, registering the model, and deploying it when the result is good enough.
  • A pipeline can include an AutoML step that creates a job to train a model automatically.
  • SageMaker Projects use templates to set up end-to-end ML solutions with continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD).
  • Processing jobs analyze and preprocess data, do feature engineering, and evaluate models.

4.2 SageMaker Model Registry

  • The Model Registry provides versioning, tracking of artifacts and lineage, an approval workflow, and cross-account support for deployment of your models.
  • A model is registered after training. Only a model that is approved moves on to deployment, so a wrong version does not go live by mistake.
  • ML Lineage Tracking records the lineage of the ML workflow, so you can answer which data and steps produced a model.

4.3 SageMaker Feature Store

  • The Feature Store is a centralized store for features and their metadata, so that features can be found and reused.
  • The online store serves low-latency, real-time inference. The offline store serves training and batch inference.
  • Because training and inference read the same feature definitions, the features are computed the same way in both, which prevents the mismatch that FlashCart saw.

4.4 Experiments and Model Cards

  • SageMaker Experiments tracks the runs of an experiment, so that you can reconstruct it and compare runs.
  • SageMaker Model Cards document a model’s information in one place, for streamlined governance and reporting through the ML lifecycle.

4.5 Which Feature for Which Need?

NeedUse
Run data preparation, training, evaluation, and deployment in orderSageMaker Pipelines
Keep versions of models and approve them before deploymentModel Registry
Use the same features in training and in inferenceFeature Store
Compare training runs, and trace how a model was madeExperiments and ML Lineage Tracking

Exam trap

Do not retrain and deploy by hand. A pipeline repeats the same steps every time, and the Model Registry keeps the versions straight. Also, a feature that is computed one way in training and another way in production gives wrong predictions: the Feature Store gives both the same definition.

Memory hook

Pipelines run the steps. The registry remembers the models. The feature store keeps the inputs the same.

5. Architecture and Cost

Problem Statement

FlashCart is ready to put the churn model to work. The website needs a live score for the customer on the page, marketing needs a nightly list of at-risk customers, and finance has noticed that the experimental endpoints from the last project are still running.

The finance team asks how SageMaker AI is billed, and how to keep the cost in check. The data team asks whether the nightly scoring needs an always-on endpoint.

The architect must draw the design and answer the cost questions.

Your challenge: Design the solution. Your answer should:

  • Show how data becomes a model, and how the model serves the website and the nightly scoring.
  • Say which parts are billed and when.
  • Name three ways to cut the cost.

5.1 A Churn Prediction Architecture

  1. The customer history is stored in Amazon S3.
  2. A SageMaker AI training job trains the model and saves the model artifact in S3.
  3. The model is deployed to a real-time endpoint, and an AWS Lambda function calls it so that the FlashCart application can show a live churn score.
  4. A nightly batch transform job scores every customer and writes the results to S3, with no endpoint needed.
  5. A SageMaker pipeline reruns the training and registers the new model version in the Model Registry, so that only an approved version is deployed.

5.2 The Cost Model

What you useHow it is billed
Training jobsFor the training time on the instances that you choose. The instances stop when the job ends.
Real-time endpointsFor the instances for as long as the endpoint is up, whether or not anything calls it.
Serverless inferenceFor what you use, not for idle time.
Asynchronous inferenceFor the instances, which can scale down to zero when the queue is empty.
Batch transformFor the job. Nothing stays on afterward.
StorageFor the data and the model artifacts in Amazon S3.
  • Use managed Spot training for flexible training jobs, to save up to 90 percent.
  • Match the hosting option to the traffic: batch transform for offline scoring, serverless for occasional calls, and a real-time endpoint only for steady, low-latency traffic.
  • Use Inference Recommender to pick the instance type, and a multi-model endpoint to share instances among models.
  • Delete endpoints and other resources that you no longer use, such as the experimental endpoints from an earlier project.
  • Start from a JumpStart model or from Canvas and Autopilot when they are enough, which saves data science time.
  • Prices change. Use the pricing page and the AWS Pricing Calculator for current numbers.
Exam trap

A real-time endpoint that nobody calls still costs money while it is up. Delete test endpoints, and use batch transform or serverless inference for offline or occasional work. Also remember that automatic model tuning and Spot training change the cost of training in opposite directions: tuning runs many jobs, and Spot lowers the price of each.

Memory hook

Pay for the job, not for idle time.

6.5 Lesson Summary

  • Amazon SageMaker AI is a fully managed service to build, train, and deploy custom ML models at scale. Use a pre-trained service when one fits the task, and SageMaker AI when you need a model of your own.
  • The workflow is: labeled data in Amazon S3, train, test, tune, deploy, and request inferences. Studio is for code, Canvas for no code, Autopilot for AutoML, and JumpStart for pre-trained models and solutions.
  • A training job runs a container on managed instances, reads data from Amazon S3, and saves the model artifact to S3. Automatic model tuning finds the best hyperparameters, and managed Spot training can cut the cost by up to 90 percent.
  • Real-time endpoints serve sustained low-latency traffic, serverless inference serves intermittent traffic, asynchronous inference handles large payloads and long jobs, and batch transform scores datasets offline.
  • Pipelines automate the steps, the Model Registry versions and approves models, and the Feature Store gives training and inference the same features.
  • Pay for the job, not for idle time: delete unused endpoints and match the hosting option to the traffic.

7. Knowledge Check

Answer each question in your own words before you open the model answer.

  1. Explain what Amazon SageMaker AI is, and when you would choose it over a pre-trained service.

    Show the model answer

    SageMaker AI is a fully managed service to build, train, and deploy custom ML models at scale. Choose it when the task is specific to your data, such as predicting churn, and no pre-trained service does it. Use a pre-trained service, such as Rekognition or Comprehend, when the task matches, because it needs no training data and no ML work.

  2. Describe the machine learning workflow in SageMaker AI.

    Show the model answer

    Prepare labeled data in Amazon S3, train a model on it, test the trained model on data that it has not seen, and tune it if the results are not good enough, by changing features, hyperparameters, or data. Then deploy it to an endpoint, and applications send inference requests and get predictions.

  3. What are labels, features, hyperparameters, and inference?

    Show the model answer

    Features are the facts about each example, and labels are the right answers. Hyperparameters are the settings of the algorithm that you tune. Inference is using a trained model to make a prediction on new data.

  4. Which environment would you give to a coder, to an analyst, and to someone who wants to start from an existing model?

    Show the model answer

    A coder uses SageMaker Studio, with JupyterLab or Code Editor. An analyst with no code uses SageMaker Canvas, and someone with no ML knowledge can use Autopilot for classification and regression. To start from an existing model, use JumpStart, which has pre-trained models and one-click solutions that you can deploy or fine-tune.

  5. How does a training job work, and what does it produce?

    Show the model answer

    The data is in Amazon S3, and the algorithm is a container image, from Amazon ECR. SageMaker AI provisions the instances, runs the job, and saves the model artifact in S3. The instances stop when the job ends, and you pay for the training time.

  6. What are the three ways to choose the algorithm?

    Show the model answer

    Built-in algorithms, where AWS provides the algorithm in a container, such as XGBoost or Linear Learner. Script mode, where you provide a script and SageMaker AI provides a framework container such as TensorFlow or PyTorch. And bring your own container, where you provide the image in Amazon ECR.

  7. What does automatic model tuning do?

    Show the model answer

    It runs many training jobs with different hyperparameter values, within ranges that you set, and finds the best configuration. It replaces tuning by hand. Because it runs many jobs, it multiplies the training cost, and it can use Spot training to lower it.

  8. How does managed Spot training cut cost, and what must you watch?

    Show the model answer

    It trains on spare EC2 capacity, which can lower the cost by up to 90 percent compared with on-demand instances. Spot capacity can be taken back, so a job can be interrupted. Use checkpoints so that a job resumes from the last checkpoint, and use it for training that can wait.

  9. Compare real-time, serverless, asynchronous, and batch transform inference.

    Show the model answer

    Real-time is a persistent endpoint for sustained, low-latency traffic. Serverless suits intermittent traffic, with payloads up to 4 MB and 60 seconds, and no idle cost. Asynchronous queues requests with payloads up to 1 GB and processing up to one hour, and can scale to zero. Batch transform scores a large dataset offline with no persistent endpoint.

  10. A model is called a few times a day and its endpoint is expensive. What do you change?

    Show the model answer

    Move it to serverless inference, which suits intermittent or unpredictable traffic, and you pay for what you use and not for idle time. Delete the always-on endpoint that nobody uses.

  11. What do a multi-model endpoint, Inference Recommender, and shadow tests do?

    Show the model answer

    A multi-model endpoint hosts many models behind one real-time endpoint so they share instances. Inference Recommender gives recommendations on instance types and configurations. Shadow tests compare a change to your model-serving setup with the current one before you switch.

  12. How do Pipelines, the Model Registry, and the Feature Store work together?

    Show the model answer

    A pipeline runs the steps in order: process, train, evaluate, and register. The Model Registry versions the models and holds the approval before deployment. The Feature Store gives training and inference the same feature definitions, with an online store for low latency and an offline store for training and batch inference.

  13. Draw an architecture that serves a churn score on a website and scores all customers nightly.

    Show the model answer

    Customer history in Amazon S3 feeds a SageMaker training job, which saves a model artifact in S3. The model is deployed to a real-time endpoint, and a Lambda function calls it for the website. A nightly batch transform job scores every customer and writes the results to S3, with no endpoint. A pipeline retrains and registers new versions, and only an approved version is deployed.

  14. How is SageMaker AI billed, and what are three ways to cut the cost?

    Show the model answer

    You pay for training time, for real-time endpoint instances while they are up, for what serverless inference uses, for the job in batch transform, and for storage in S3. Use Spot training for flexible jobs, match the hosting option to the traffic, use Inference Recommender and multi-model endpoints, and delete endpoints that you no longer use.
