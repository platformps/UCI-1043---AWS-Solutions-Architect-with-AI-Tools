Amazon Transcribe

How Amazon Transcribe converts speech to text: batch and streaming transcription, language and speaker identification, custom vocabularies and language models, redaction and toxicity detection, and the architecture, security, and cost of a speech pipeline.

Lesson Overview

Amazon Transcribe is a managed speech recognition service. It converts speech to text with automatic speech recognition (ASR), a deep learning technology, and you call it through an application programming interface (API), so you need no machine learning (ML) expertise. This lesson follows FlashCart Inc., a fictional online marketplace that records customer service calls, publishes product videos, and wants to search and analyze what is said in them. In this lesson, you will study:

  • Introduction to Amazon Transcribe: what the service does, its inputs and languages, and common use cases.
  • Batch and Streaming Transcription: recorded files versus live audio, language identification, speaker diarization, and channel identification.
  • Customizing Transcripts: custom vocabulary, vocabulary filters, and custom language models.
  • Safety, Call Analytics, and Medical: redaction of personally identifiable information (PII), toxicity detection, Transcribe Call Analytics, and Transcribe Medical.
  • Architecture, Security, and Cost: pipelines that combine Transcribe with other AWS artificial intelligence (AI) services, data privacy, and the pricing model.
  • Comparison and Scenarios: how Transcribe compares with other AWS AI services, and an SAA-C03 cheat sheet.
  • Knowledge Check: open-ended questions with model answers.

1. Introduction to Amazon Transcribe

Problem Statement

FlashCart Inc. records every customer service call and publishes hundreds of product videos. Nobody can search what was said in them, and the videos have no captions. A person who typed out one call needed an hour for ten minutes of audio.

The engineering manager wants every recording turned into text, with captions for the videos. The team has no speech scientists or ML engineers. She asks what the service can read, how long a recording can be, and how to make sure that the text can be used for search and analysis.

She also wants to know whether the accuracy depends on the recording.

Your challenge: Advise the manager. Your answer should:

  • Explain what Transcribe does and why it needs no ML expertise.
  • State the inputs, languages, and limits.
  • Say what affects the accuracy of the result.

1.1 What Amazon Transcribe Is

  • Amazon Transcribe is an AWS AI service that makes it easy to convert speech to text. It uses automatic speech recognition (ASR), a deep learning process.
  • Typical business uses are transcribing voice-based customer service calls, generating subtitles for audio and video, and running text-based analysis on audio and video content.
  • It is a cloud-based, managed service. You call the API, and you do not provision or manage any infrastructure.
  • You can start in the AWS Management Console by submitting a job for an audio file, call it from the AWS Command Line Interface (AWS CLI), or use an AWS software development kit (SDK).
  • It sits in the AI services layer, with Amazon Rekognition, Amazon Textract, Amazon Comprehend, and Amazon Translate, which you studied in Lessons 299.1 to 299.4.

1.2 From Audio to Text

The text output can feed other services. For example, Amazon Comprehend can find sentiment, entities, and key phrases in a transcript. Amazon Translate and Amazon Polly can turn voice input in one language into voice output in another language.

1.3 How You Use Transcribe

  • Batch transcription: you transcribe audio or video files that are stored in Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3).
  • Streaming transcription: you send live audio and get text back in real time.
  • Both batch and streaming output come in JSON format.
  • Transcribe supports single-channel and dual-channel audio and video for both batch and streaming.
  • Supported media types differ between batch and streaming, and lossless formats are recommended for both.

1.4 Languages and Features

  • Transcribe supports more than 100 languages, for example English, Chinese, Arabic, French, Korean, Hindi, and German.
  • Not every feature works in every language. Before you start, check the supported languages page for the language in your media and for the feature that you want to use.

1.5 Limits

  • A batch job can process an audio file of up to eight hours (28,800 seconds) and up to 2 GB. These two limits are fixed.
  • A file must be at least 500 milliseconds long.
  • A streaming connection can stay open for up to eight hours. A stream that goes over the maximum session duration fails, and you start a new session.
  • Defaults for an account in a Region: 250 concurrent batch jobs, 25 concurrent streams, and 25 requests per second to start a batch job. You can ask for an increase in the Service Quotas console.
  • Job records are kept for 90 days.
  • Limits change, so check the Transcribe documentation for the current values.

1.6 What Affects Accuracy

  • Transcribe is designed to handle a wide range of speech, including differences in volume, pitch, and speaking rate.
  • The quality and content of the audio can still affect the result: background noise, overlapping speakers, accented speech, and switches between languages inside one file.
  • It works with almost any device that has a microphone, such as phones, computers, tablets, and Internet of Things (IoT) devices.

2. Batch and Streaming Transcription

Problem Statement

FlashCart has two transcription needs. The compliance team wants every recorded call transcribed overnight. The support team wants live captions on a customer’s call while the agent is still talking.

The calls are recorded on two channels, one for the agent and one for the customer, and the compliance team wants to know who said what. Some calls are in languages that the system does not know in advance. Video interviews with several people on one channel must also show who is speaking.

The architect must choose the transcription mode and the features that label languages and speakers.

Your challenge: Choose the mode and the features. Your answer should:

  • Say which mode fits recorded calls and which fits live captions.
  • Name the feature for an unknown language.
  • Explain how to label who said what on a two-channel call and on a one-channel recording.

2.1 Batch Transcription

  • Batch transcription processes a media file that is stored in an Amazon S3 bucket. You start a job, the job runs asynchronously, and you read the transcript when it is finished.
  • You check on a job with GetTranscriptionJob. Amazon EventBridge can also react when a job changes state: Transcribe sends a Transcribe Job State Change event with the job name and the new status, COMPLETED or FAILED. The event does not carry the transcript, so your code reads the transcript from the output location. EventBridge events are sent on a best-effort basis.
  • You can save the transcript in your own S3 bucket. If you do not name an output bucket, Transcribe uses a service-managed bucket, and you get a temporary link that works for 15 minutes. You can ask for a new one with GetTranscriptionJob.
  • It fits recorded content: call archives, interviews, videos, and voicemail.

2.2 Streaming Transcription

  • With streaming, you send an audio stream to the service and receive a text stream back in real time. You can use an AWS SDK, which AWS prefers, an HTTP/2 stream, a WebSocket, or the console with your microphone.
  • It fits live content: live captions for events, live call monitoring, and voice interfaces. Streaming is faster, but the speed can limit accuracy in some cases.
  • Streaming accepts FLAC, Opus audio in an Ogg container, and PCM audio, which must be signed 16-bit little-endian. WAV is not included. Lossless formats are recommended.
  • Streaming does not work with every language. Check the Data input column of the supported languages table.
  • Best practices: use PCM, record at 16,000 Hz if you can, send chunks of 50 to 200 milliseconds in a uniform size, state the number of channels correctly, and send silence while nobody speaks.
  • Streaming supports language identification, speaker diarization, custom vocabularies, custom language models, vocabulary filters, and PII redaction. Some options differ from batch, so check the documentation.

2.3 Language Identification

  • Transcribe can automatically identify the dominant language in the audio and then transcribe it. It works for batch and streaming.
  • It can identify any language that Transcribe supports. The result includes the identified language and a confidence score.
  • You can give a list of languages that might be in your media. The service then chooses from that list, and the accuracy is better than when it searches all the languages.
  • Batch jobs also support multi-language identification, for a file that has more than one language.

2.5 Subtitles

  • Transcribe can generate subtitles for a batch video job in WebVTT (.vtt) or SubRip (.srt) format, or both. It also produces the regular transcript file.
  • You choose the number of the first subtitle, 0 or 1. The default is 0, and 1 is more widely used, so AWS suggests 1 for better compatibility with other services.
  • The subtitle files are stored in the same Amazon S3 location as the transcript.
  • Subtitles appear at the same time as the words are spoken, and they stay visible until a natural pause or until the speaker finishes.
  • You can then translate the subtitles into other languages with Amazon Translate (Lesson 299.4).
Exam trap

Diarization and channel identification are different. Diarization separates speakers who share one channel, and channel identification uses separate channels. Neither one tells you the name of the person: they label Speaker 0 or channel 0. Also, streaming and batch are not interchangeable: a recorded file in Amazon S3 is a batch job, and live audio is a stream.

Memory hook

A file in S3 is batch. A live voice is a stream.

3. Customizing Transcripts

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s first transcripts are readable, but they get the company’s own words wrong. “FlashCart Express” is transcribed as “flash cart express,” product codes such as “FX-200” are missing, and a seller’s name is spelled three different ways.

The legal team also wants some words never to appear in the text of public captions. And the training team has a large library of transcripts of FlashCart’s own webinars, full of internal jargon, that they hope can teach the system how FlashCart speaks.

The architect knows that Transcribe has three tools for this, and she must pick the right one for each problem.

Your challenge: Choose the right tool for each problem. Your answer should:

  • Name the tool for a short list of names and codes.
  • Name the tool that keeps unwanted words out of captions.
  • Name the tool that learns from a large body of text, and say what training data it needs.

3.1 Custom Vocabulary

  • A custom vocabulary adds new words to the base vocabulary, so that Transcribe produces more accurate transcriptions of domain-specific words and phrases, such as brand names, product codes, and acronyms.
  • It works for batch and streaming transcription.
  • Quotas per account in a Region: a vocabulary can be up to 51,200 bytes, a phrase up to 256 characters, and you can keep up to 100 vocabularies. Most of these can be raised, but the phrase length cannot.
  • It is not a guarantee: the output depends on other factors too, so a term in the list can still be missed.
  • If a word is not recognized, it is often pronounced very differently from how it is written. Add several phrase entries for the same word, and use the DisplayAs column for the output that you want.
  • If too many custom words appear in the output, the list is probably too long. Keep a small list of rare words that you expect, and split a large list into lists for different use cases.
  • Short words that sound like many other words can over-produce. Join them with the words around them, as hyphen-separated phrases.

3.2 Vocabulary Filters

  • A vocabulary filter is automatic moderation of the output. You give a list of words, and Transcribe masks or removes them in the transcript.
  • Both batch and streaming transcription support vocabulary filters.
  • An account can have up to 100 filters in a Region, and each filter can be up to 50 KB.
  • Use it for unwanted words in public captions and in transcripts that many people read.

3.3 Custom Language Models

  • A custom language model (CLM) is a domain-specific language model that you train with your own text data. It benefits organizations that have a lot of internal jargon or a specialized technical field.
  • You supply the text in an Amazon S3 bucket, and then use the Transcribe console to train the model. Training is automated.
  • Training data: at least 100,000 and at most 10 million words of running text that is relevant to your audio. Use UTF-8, with one sentence per line and punctuation in each sentence. You check the spelling and the encoding.
  • Training usually takes 6 to 10 hours. Afterward, the model is available in your account, and you add its model ID to a transcription request.
  • By default, an account can have 3 models training at once and 10 models in total in a Region. You can ask for increases in the Service Quotas console.
  • Improvements are not guaranteed. In some tests, accuracy improved by as much as 10 to 15 percent relative to the base model. Test the model on your own audio.
  • CLM supports Australian English, British English, Hindi, US English, and US Spanish for batch transcription, and US English for streaming. Custom acoustic models are not supported.
  • You own the original text and the model. Transcribe does not store the training text and does not use it to improve the general engine.

4. Safety, Call Analytics, and Medical

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s call transcripts include names, addresses, and card numbers. Analysts should not see them, and the transcripts must not hold personal data. The security officer also asks whether the audio itself can be cleaned.

The community team records English-language seller voice chats and wants harmful language flagged for a person to review. The contact center director wants more than text: she wants customer and agent sentiment, the reasons that customers call, and a summary of each call.

FlashCart’s healthcare partner wants to transcribe clinicians’ notes and conversations, and asks which service is made for that.

Your challenge: Choose the right capability for each need. Your answer should:

  • Say how to redact personal data, and what redaction does not do.
  • Name the capability that flags harmful language, and the one that gives call insights.
  • Name the service for medical speech.

4.1 PII Redaction

  • Automatic content redaction identifies and removes personally identifiable information (PII) from transcripts. It works for batch and streaming.
  • It does not remove the personal information from the source audio. It gives you start and end timestamps for each PII phrase, so that you can redact the audio yourself.
  • It works only with audio as input. It cannot redact PII in a text transcript that you already have.
  • Because machine learning is predictive, it may miss some PII. Review the output to make sure that it meets your needs.
  • Streaming has two extras: you can identify PII without redacting it, and you can choose specific PII types, for example only social security numbers and card numbers.
  • Language and Region availability differs, so check the documentation.

4.2 Toxicity Detection

  • Toxicity detection finds and classifies toxic speech. It is designed to help moderate peer-to-peer dialogue, such as online gaming and social chat, and it uses audio cues, such as tone and pitch, as well as the text.
  • It uses seven categories: profanity, hate speech, sexual, insult, violence or threat, graphic, and harassment or abuse.
  • It analyzes speech segments, which are the speech between natural pauses. Each segment gets a score between 0 and 1 for each category, and an overall toxicity score. A higher score means a greater likelihood of toxic speech, so you choose a threshold for your use case.
  • It is available only for batch transcription in US English (en-US). Other job options are not supported with it, but you can turn on PII redaction in the same job.
  • It has additional charges.
  • It does not decide what to do. You choose the action, such as sending a clip to a moderator.

4.3 Transcribe Call Analytics

  • Transcribe Call Analytics is a separate API that adds conversation insights to transcripts, trained for customer care and outbound sales calls.
  • It works for post-call analytics, which is batch, and for real-time analytics, which is streaming. Real-time analytics is not available in every Region that supports post-call analytics.
  • A batch job can use a file of up to four hours and 500 MB, with two channels.
  • It gives customer and agent sentiment scores, call drivers, call categories, and call summarization. Call summarization works for English dialects, and issue detection works for Australian, British, and US English.
  • Common uses are agent assist, summarization, supervisor alerts, and call analytics.
  • It removes sensitive personal information from both the transcripts and the source audio.
  • It is priced separately from the standard Transcribe APIs. Generative call summarization is available only for post-call analytics and only in some Regions, so check the documentation.

4.4 Transcribe Medical

  • Transcribe Medical is an ASR service for medical speech: dictation by one person, and conversations between several people.
  • It supports medical transcription in US English, and it supports many medical specialties. Some specialties, such as cardiology, neurology, oncology, and radiology, are for streaming only, and primary care specialties work for batch and streaming.
  • A medical batch job can use a file of up to four hours and 2 GB.
  • Typical uses are clinical documentation, recording physician notes, and drug safety monitoring.
  • It is HIPAA eligible, and AWS says that it does not use the content for anything other than providing the service.
  • It is not a substitute for professional medical advice. It may not identify all protected health information, and it does not meet the HIPAA requirements for de-identification, so people must review the output.

5. Architecture, Security, and Cost

Problem Statement

FlashCart wants every call recording transcribed within minutes of upload, with personal data removed and the sentiment of the call stored. The marketing team also dreams of a voice assistant that listens in one language and answers in another.

The security team asks what AWS does with the audio and what can be deleted. The finance team asks how the service is billed, and why Call Analytics shows up as a separate line.

The architect must draw the designs and answer both teams.

Your challenge: Design the solution. Your answer should:

  • Show how a recording leads to a redacted transcript and an analysis.
  • Show how Transcribe, Translate, and Polly work together.
  • Describe data privacy, deletion, and the billing model.

5.1 A Pipeline for Recordings

  1. A recording is saved to an Amazon S3 bucket.
  2. The upload event starts an AWS Lambda function, which starts an Amazon Transcribe job with PII redaction turned on.
  3. When the job status changes to COMPLETED or FAILED, an Amazon EventBridge rule starts a second Lambda function. The event carries the job name and the status, not the transcript.
  4. The function reads the redacted transcript from the output location, and sends it to Amazon Comprehend for sentiment, entities, and key phrases (Lesson 299.3).
  5. The function stores the results, for example in Amazon DynamoDB.

To make an audio and video library searchable, index the transcripts in a search service such as Amazon OpenSearch Service.

5.2 Speech to Speech in Another Language

  • Transcribe accepts the voice input, Translate converts the text to another language, and Polly produces the voice output. This enables multilingual conversations.
  • Transcribe and Translate also produce subtitles in many languages (Lesson 299.4).

5.3 Security and Data Privacy

  • Use AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM) policies and roles to control who can start jobs and read transcripts, and who can read the media in Amazon S3.
  • AWS says that it protects content with encryption at rest and in transit, and that only authorized employees can access it.
  • Transcribe may store and use voice inputs to provide the service and, unless you opt out, to improve Transcribe and other Amazon AI technologies. To opt out, use an AWS Organizations AI services opt-out policy.
  • Content is encrypted and stored at rest in the Region where you use Transcribe. A portion may be stored in another Region for improvement of the service, unless you opt out.
  • You always own your content. You can delete the data and artifacts of a transcription job with the delete APIs, such as DeleteTranscriptionJob, and you can ask AWS Support to delete voice inputs.
  • For custom language models, the training text is not stored after the model is built, and the model belongs to you and is available only to you.
  • Transcribe Medical does not use customer content to improve the service.

5.4 Quotas and Throttling

  • Defaults for an account in a Region: 250 concurrent batch jobs, 25 concurrent streams, and 25 requests per second to start a batch job or a stream. Most can be raised in the Service Quotas console.
  • A LimitExceededException on a stream has three causes. The most common is exceeding the concurrent stream quota: retry with exponential backoff, and request an increase if you hit it often.
  • The second cause is a stream that goes over the maximum session duration. This limit cannot be increased, so you start a new streaming session.
  • The third, which is rare, is raising the number of concurrent streams too quickly, for example in a load test. Retry with backoff and increase the number of streams gradually.
  • Job records are kept for 90 days. Store the transcripts that you need to keep.

5.5 The Cost Model

  • Transcribe is priced by the amount of audio that you transcribe. Check the pricing page for current rates.
  • Call Analytics and Transcribe Medical are priced separately from standard transcription, and toxicity detection has additional charges. Read the pricing page for the features that you turn on.
  • Transcribe once and store the transcript, instead of transcribing the same recording again.
  • Use batch jobs for archives. Use streaming only when you need the text while the person is still speaking.
  • Delete custom models that you no longer need.
Exam trap

6.5 Lesson Summary

  • Amazon Transcribe is a managed ASR service in the AI services layer. It converts speech to text in more than 100 languages with no ML expertise, and it outputs JSON, and subtitles on request.
  • Batch transcription handles recorded files in Amazon S3, up to eight hours and 2 GB. Streaming transcription returns text in real time, and accepts FLAC, Opus, and PCM audio.
  • Language identification handles unknown languages. Speaker diarization labels speakers on one channel, and channel identification labels separate channels. Neither says who a person is.
  • Custom vocabulary fixes a few terms, vocabulary filters keep words out, and custom language models learn a domain from at least 100,000 words of text.
  • PII redaction cleans the transcript but not the audio, and toxicity detection flags harmful speech in US English batch jobs. Call Analytics and Transcribe Medical are separate capabilities with their own prices.
  • Transcribe may store and use audio to improve the service unless you opt out. It combines with Comprehend, Translate, and Polly.
Memory hook

Pictures go to Rekognition. Documents go to Textract. Speech goes to Transcribe. Text goes to Comprehend or Translate.

7. Knowledge Check

Answer each question in your own words before you open the model answer.

  1. Explain what Amazon Transcribe is, and why FlashCart can use it with no ML engineers.

    Show the model answer

    Transcribe is a managed automatic speech recognition service that converts speech to text. FlashCart calls an API, or uses the console, and AWS runs the deep learning models and the infrastructure, so no speech or ML expertise is needed.

  2. Which inputs, languages, and limits does Transcribe support?

    Show the model answer

    It transcribes recorded audio and video files in Amazon S3 and live audio streams, in more than 100 languages. A batch file can be up to eight hours and 2 GB, and a streaming connection can stay open for up to eight hours. Features vary by language, so check the supported languages page.

  3. What can lower the accuracy of a transcript?

    Show the model answer

    Background noise, overlapping speakers, accented speech, and switches between languages inside one file. A lossless format is recommended for the best quality.

  4. Compare batch and streaming transcription.

    Show the model answer

    Batch transcribes a recorded file in Amazon S3 as an asynchronous job, and you read the transcript when the job finishes. Streaming opens a bidirectional stream over HTTP/2, so you send live audio and get text back in real time. Use batch for archives and overnight work, and streaming for live captions and live assistance.

  5. How do you handle recordings in languages that you do not know in advance?

    Show the model answer

    Use automatic language identification, which works for batch and streaming. Transcribe identifies the dominant language and returns it with a confidence score. Give a list of languages that might be present to improve accuracy. Batch jobs also support multi-language identification.

  6. Compare speaker diarization with channel identification.

    Show the model answer

    Speaker diarization separates different speakers on one channel by their voice characteristics and labels them, for example Speaker 0 and Speaker 1, with up to 30 speakers. Channel identification transcribes each channel of a multi-channel recording and returns one transcript with channel labels. Neither says who the person is.

  7. When would you use a custom vocabulary, a vocabulary filter, or a custom language model?

    Show the model answer

    Use a custom vocabulary for a short list of names, codes, and acronyms, with DisplayAs for the output that you want. Use a vocabulary filter to mask or remove unwanted words. Use a custom language model to learn a whole domain from at least 100,000 words of text. None of them guarantees a result.

  8. What does PII redaction do, and what are its limits?

    Show the model answer

    It identifies and removes PII from the transcript, for batch and streaming. It does not remove PII from the source audio, but it gives timestamps so that you can. It works only on audio input, and it may miss some PII, so review the output. Streaming can also identify without redacting, and can target specific PII types.

  9. How do toxicity detection and Call Analytics differ?

    Show the model answer

    Toxicity detection flags harmful speech in seven categories, such as profanity, hate speech, and violence or threat, for a person to review. It works only for US English batch jobs. Call Analytics is a separate API for contact center calls. It adds sentiment scores, call drivers, categories, and summaries, and it removes sensitive information from the transcript and the audio. It is priced separately.

  10. When would you choose Transcribe Medical, and what should you remember about it?

    Show the model answer

    Use it for medical dictation and clinician-patient conversations, such as physician notes. It supports US English and is HIPAA eligible. It is not a substitute for professional judgment, it may not identify all protected health information, and it does not meet the HIPAA de-identification requirements, so people must review the output.

  11. Draw a pipeline that transcribes recordings, removes PII, and stores the sentiment.

    Show the model answer

    A recording is saved to Amazon S3. An S3 event starts a Lambda function that starts a Transcribe job with PII redaction. When the job state changes, an EventBridge rule on the job status starts a second Lambda function, which reads the redacted transcript from the output location and sends it to Amazon Comprehend. The function stores the results in Amazon DynamoDB.

  12. How can you build a voice that listens in one language and answers in another?

    Show the model answer

    Chain Transcribe, Translate, and Polly. Transcribe converts the speech to text, Translate converts the text to the target language, and Polly converts the translated text to speech.

  13. What does AWS do with the audio that Transcribe processes, and how can you delete it or opt out?

    Show the model answer

    Transcribe may store and use voice inputs to provide the service and, unless you opt out, to improve Transcribe and other Amazon AI technologies. You own your content. Opt out with an AWS Organizations AI services opt-out policy. You can delete a job and its data with the delete APIs, and ask AWS Support to delete voice inputs.

  14. How is Transcribe billed, and how can you control cost?

    Show the model answer

    It is priced by the amount of audio transcribed, with separate prices for Call Analytics and Transcribe Medical, and additional charges for features such as toxicity detection. Transcribe once and store the transcript, use batch jobs for archives, and delete custom models that you do not need.
