AWS Quick

Turn data into dashboards: how AWS Quick Sight connects to data, uses SPICE and direct query, controls access, and compares with the other analytics services.

Overview and Objectives

Lesson Overview

AWS Analytics Services

Every service in this module so far moves, stores, or queries data. The last step is to show the data to the people who decide. Executives do not write SQL, and they do not read raw files. They need charts that they can filter, and dashboards that update. This lesson covers the AWS capability that does that work: AWS Quick Sight, the business intelligence part of AWS Quick.

Quick Sight connects to your data, lets authors build interactive visuals and dashboards, and shares them with readers. It is serverless, so there are no servers to run. This lesson explains what AWS Quick and Quick Sight are, how data gets into a dataset, the difference between SPICE and direct query, how security and sharing work, and how Quick Sight compares with the other services that you know. The last two tabs prepare you for the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate (SAA-C03) exam and check what you learned.

A note on names: older AWS pages and exam material call the business intelligence service Amazon QuickSight, and some pages call the wider product Amazon Quick Suite. Pages from before November 9, 2026 call them Amazon Quick and Amazon Quick Sight. The current names are AWS Quick and AWS Quick Sight.

1. Introduction to AWS Quick

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s sales data lives in several places. The orders are files in Amazon S3, a warehouse in Amazon Redshift holds the finance data, and the product search runs on Amazon OpenSearch Service. An analyst exports numbers into a spreadsheet each week, and the leadership team reads it on Monday.

The leaders want interactive dashboards that they can filter themselves, on current data, on any device. The company does not want to manage servers for this.

Your challenge:

  • Choose a service that builds dashboards on data from several sources.
  • Explain how data flows from the data lake to a dashboard.
  • Name the terms that authors and readers use.

1.1 Introduction to AWS Quick

  • AWS Quick is an AI-powered workplace service with six integrated capabilities: AWS Quick Sight for data visualization and business intelligence, AWS Quick Flows for workflow automation, AWS Quick Automate for business process automation, AWS Quick Index for data discovery, AWS Quick Research for in-depth research, and Apps in Quick.
  • Chat is the main interface of Quick, and spaces organize the context that the chat uses.
  • This lesson focuses on AWS Quick Sight, the business intelligence capability. It is the part that the SAA-C03 exam asks about.
  • Quick Sight dashboards and Quick Automate are available in accounts that you provision through the AWS Management Console. They are not available in the Free and Plus accounts that you create at aws.com/quick.
  • Older AWS pages call the product Amazon Quick Suite or, before November 9, 2026, Amazon Quick, and older exam material calls the business intelligence service Amazon QuickSight. They all point to the same service.

1.2 AWS Quick Sight

  • Business intelligence (BI) is the practice of turning data into charts, reports, and dashboards that help people decide.
  • Quick Sight is a scalable, serverless, machine learning (ML)-powered BI service. It is fast, automatically scalable, and embeddable, and readers can be billed per session.
  • Authors use it to build visualizations and run ad hoc analysis. Readers use the published dashboards to get business insights on any device.
  • It scales to hundreds of thousands of users, and it uses an in-memory engine called SPICE for fast, responsive queries (Tab 2).
  • Common uses are self-service business intelligence, executive dashboards, and embedded analytics in websites and applications.

  • Files in Amazon S3 that are compressed with zip or gzip can be imported as they are.

1.5 From a Data Lake to a Dashboard

  • A common pipeline stores raw files in Amazon S3, catalogs them with an AWS Glue crawler, and queries them with Amazon Athena.
  • Quick Sight then uses Athena as a data source, and an author builds a dataset, an analysis, and a dashboard on it.
  • The pipeline reuses the services that you learned in this module. Quick Sight is the last step, which shows the results.

Exam Trap

Remember these points:

  • Quick Sight is the AWS business intelligence service. Dashboards for business users, on data from many sources, with no servers, point to Quick Sight.
  • Quick Sight reads data from other services. It does not store your data lake or run your ETL jobs.
  • The names changed: QuickSight on older material is AWS Quick Sight, part of AWS Quick.
  • OpenSearch Dashboards visualizes data in OpenSearch. Quick Sight visualizes data from many sources, including OpenSearch.

2. Datasets, SPICE, and Dashboards

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s sales dashboard is slow every morning at nine, because 300 people open it at once and each visual queries the Amazon Redshift cluster. The data loads each night, so the numbers do not change during the day.

The operations team also wants one page that shows live order counts. The team must share the dashboard with the right people and keep it up to date.

Your challenge:

  • Choose between SPICE and direct query for each page.
  • Keep the SPICE data current after the nightly load.
  • Publish and share the dashboard.

2.1 From Data to Dashboard

  1. Connect to a data source, such as Amazon Athena or Amazon Redshift.
  2. Create a dataset. Shape it with field formatting, hierarchies, data type conversions, and calculations.
  3. Create an analysis from the dataset.
  4. Add visuals, and add more sheets and datasets if you need them.
  5. Make the visuals interactive with parameters, controls, filters, and custom actions.
  6. Publish the analysis as a dashboard.
  7. Share the dashboard with the people who need it.

2.2 SPICE

  • SPICE is an in-memory calculation engine that is built into Quick Sight. It uses columnar storage, in-memory technology, and machine code generation to answer queries on large datasets quickly.
  • A SPICE dataset is a snapshot of the source data. After the data is imported, Quick Sight answers the queries from the snapshot and does not connect to the source.
  • That spares the data source from repeated queries, and it makes dashboards faster.
  • You do not provision or manage any infrastructure for SPICE. It scales automatically when many people use the same dataset.
  • SPICE is billed by the gigabytes of capacity that you buy. Using the data in any number of dashboards and by any number of users does not add charges.
  • Each author license includes 10 GB of SPICE capacity, and you can buy more for the account.
  • The data in SPICE is encrypted at rest, with a key that AWS manages by default, or with your own AWS KMS key. Data in transit uses TLS.

2.3 Direct Query

  • With direct query, Quick Sight sends the queries to the data source every time a visual loads.
  • The data is near real time, because every request reads the source.
  • There is no row or size limit for the dataset, so it suits data that is larger than the SPICE limits.
  • The speed depends on the data source. A slow source gives a slow dashboard, and many readers can overwhelm it.
  • Quick Sight allows two minutes to generate a visual. A longer query times out.

2.4 SPICE and Direct Query Compared

FeatureSPICEDirect query
Where the data is readFrom a snapshot inside Quick Sight.From the data source, every time.
FreshnessAs fresh as the last refresh.Near real time.
SpeedFast, and consistent for many readers.Depends on the data source.
Load on the sourceLow. The source is queried only during a refresh.High when many readers open dashboards.
Size limitsPer-dataset limits on rows and size. Check the quotas page.No row or size limit for the dataset.
CostBilled by the SPICE capacity.No SPICE capacity. The data source bears the query cost.
Best forDashboards that need speed, on data that changes daily or hourly.Business-critical data that must always be current.
  • You can mix both modes. One dashboard can use a SPICE dataset for the daily summary page and a direct query dataset for a live page.

2.5 Refreshing SPICE Data

  • Refresh a SPICE dataset on a schedule, on demand in the console, or with the API.
  • A common pattern calls the API when the ETL job finishes, so that the dashboard refreshes right after the new data lands.
  • An incremental refresh adds only the new records, and it suits data that is mostly appended. A full refresh reloads everything.
  • Enterprise edition can refresh imported datasets every hour. Incremental refreshes can run as often as every 15 minutes.
  • While a refresh runs, the dashboards keep showing the previous snapshot, so readers are not interrupted.
  • There are daily limits on on-demand refreshes for each dataset. Check the data source quotas page.

2.6 Insights and Reports

  • Enterprise edition adds ML-powered insights: forecasting, anomaly and outlier detection, and key driver identification.
  • Narrative insights describe the data in text, with calculations that update.
  • Authors can also build paginated reports, set threshold alerts, and ask natural language questions about their data.
  • Enterprise edition can email dashboard reports to readers.
  • You can embed interactive dashboards in websites and applications, and manage datasets and dashboards with API operations.

Exam Trap

Remember these points:

  • SPICE is for speed and for sparing the data source. Direct query is for live data.
  • A SPICE dataset is a snapshot. It is only as current as its last refresh.
  • To refresh after an ETL job, call the refresh API. To refresh at fixed times, use a schedule.
  • SPICE is billed by capacity. More readers do not add SPICE charges.

3. Security, Users, and Pricing

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s regional managers must see only the orders of their own region, and only the finance team may see the margin column. The dashboards also have to reach managers who sign in with the company login.

Some data sits in a private VPC, and the finance team asks what Quick Sight will cost, because most people only read dashboards.

Your challenge:

  • Give each person the right role and the right data.
  • Reach data in a private VPC.
  • Name the cost dimensions.

3.1 Roles

RoleWhat the role does
AdminManages users, billing, and settings for the Quick account, and can do everything that an author does.
AuthorPrepares datasets, builds analyses, and publishes dashboards.
ReaderViews and interacts with published dashboards. Readers can be billed per session.
  • The Pro roles, such as Author Pro and Reader Pro, add the generative AI capabilities of AWS Quick.
  • Quick Sight keeps its own users and groups, with these roles. Access to the data inside Quick Sight is managed in Quick Sight, with sharing and with row-level and column-level security.

3.2 Identity

  • In Enterprise edition, people can sign in through AWS IAM Identity Center or AWS Directory Service for Microsoft Active Directory.
  • IAM Identity Center can hold the identities itself, or it can connect to your own identity provider, such as Microsoft Active Directory, Okta, or Microsoft Entra ID.
  • Map the groups of the identity provider to the Admin, Author, and Reader roles, and manage access by group.
  • You cannot remap users or groups from one identity store to another. You would unsubscribe and subscribe again.

3.3 Sharing

  • Publish an analysis as a dashboard, and share the dashboard with specific users or groups, with everyone in the account, or with anyone on the internet.
  • Sharing with anyone on the internet works only after an administrator turns on public sharing for the account.
  • Readers can interact with a dashboard, with filters and controls, without changing the analysis.
  • Shared folders organize the dashboards, analyses, and datasets of a team, and manage the access of the team.

3.4 Row-Level and Column-Level Security

  • Row-level security (RLS) controls which rows of a dataset each user or group can see. Column-level security (CLS) controls which columns they can see.
  • Both are available in Enterprise edition, and you set them on the dataset.
  • RLS uses rules that map each user to the values that the user may see. The next tables show the idea.

3.5 Network and Encryption

  • In Enterprise edition, you can add Quick Sight to your VPC, so it can reach data sources in a VPC and on premises.
  • To connect to on-premises data, add the data sources and a Quick-specific network interface to the VPC.
  • Quick Sight needs permission to read your AWS data sources, such as Amazon S3 and Amazon Athena. Grant the access that each source needs, and no more.
  • SPICE data is encrypted at rest, and you can use your own AWS KMS key. Data in transit uses TLS.

3.6 Quick Sight Pricing

Prices change often and differ by Region. Check the current rates on the AWS Quick Sight pricing page.

DimensionHow you are billed

ReadersA monthly subscription for each user, or pay per session with reader capacity pricing.
SPICEBy the gigabytes of capacity. Each author license includes 10 GB, and you can buy more.
EditionsStandard and Enterprise. Enterprise adds VPC access, row-level and column-level security, hourly refresh, and ML insights.
  • Pay-per-session reader pricing suits many people who open dashboards only now and then.

Exam Trap

Remember these points:

  • Row-level security is the answer when each user must see different rows of the same dataset. It needs Enterprise edition.
  • Column-level security hides columns, such as a margin field, from some users.
  • To reach data in a VPC or on premises, add Quick Sight to the VPC (Enterprise edition).
  • Reader capacity pricing (per session) suits many occasional readers.

4.2 When to Use Quick Sight

4.2.1 Use It When

  • Business users need interactive dashboards on data from AWS services, other databases, files, or SaaS sources.
  • You want no servers to run, and you want the service to scale with the number of readers.
  • You need row-level or column-level security, ML insights, or embedded analytics.

4.2.2 Use Another Service When

  • Amazon Athena or Amazon Redshift: you need to query or store the data. Quick Sight only visualizes it.
  • AWS Glue: you need to clean and transform the data first.
  • OpenSearch Dashboards: the data is only in OpenSearch, and the audience is the operations team.

4.4 Lesson Summary

This lesson covered the following points:

  • AWS Quick is a workplace service, and AWS Quick Sight is its serverless business intelligence capability. Older material calls it Amazon QuickSight.
  • Quick Sight reads data from AWS stores, other databases, files, and SaaS sources. A data lake pipeline ends in a Quick Sight dashboard through Athena.
  • A dataset uses SPICE, a refreshable in-memory snapshot, or direct query, which asks the source every time.
  • Authors build analyses and publish dashboards. Readers interact with them. Admins manage the account.

  • Pricing combines user subscriptions, optional per-session reader pricing, and SPICE capacity.

5. Knowledge Check

Answer each question in your own words before you open the model answer. The questions were written for this course.

Question 1: Explain what AWS Quick Sight is, how it relates to AWS Quick, and why older material uses a different name.

Maps to: Objective 1 (Comprehension).

AWS Quick Sight is a serverless, machine learning-powered business intelligence service. It connects to data sources, lets authors build visualizations and dashboards, and lets readers use them. It is the business intelligence capability of AWS Quick, which also includes Quick Flows, Quick Automate, Quick Index, Quick Research, and Apps in Quick. Older AWS pages and exam material call the same service Amazon QuickSight, and some pages call the wider product Amazon Quick Suite or, before November 9, 2026, Amazon Quick.

Question 2: Describe the path from raw files in Amazon S3 to a Quick Sight dashboard for FlashCart.

Maps to: Objective 2 (Application).

Store the raw files in Amazon S3. Run an AWS Glue crawler to add the files to the Data Catalog. Use Amazon Athena to query the tables with SQL. In Quick Sight, connect Athena as a data source, create a dataset, build an analysis with visuals, and publish it as a dashboard. Share the dashboard with the leadership team.

Question 3: Compare SPICE and direct query, and choose a mode for a daily summary page and for a live order count page.

Maps to: Objective 3 (Analysis).

SPICE keeps a refreshable snapshot of the data in Quick Sight. It is fast, it spares the data source, and it is only as current as the last refresh. Direct query asks the data source every time, so the data is near real time, and the speed depends on the source. Use SPICE for the daily summary page, because the data changes once a day and many people open it. Use direct query for the live order count page, because it must always be current. One dashboard can use both.

Question 4: Explain how you would keep a SPICE dataset current after a nightly ETL job, and what readers see during the refresh.

Maps to: Objective 4 (Comprehension).

Call the Quick Sight refresh API when the ETL job finishes, or schedule a refresh for a time after the job usually ends. For data that is mostly appended, use an incremental refresh instead of a full refresh. While the refresh runs, the dashboards keep showing the previous snapshot, so readers are not interrupted.

Question 5: FlashCart’s regional managers must see only their own region, and only finance may see the margin column. Describe how you would configure Quick Sight.

Maps to: Objective 5 (Application).

Use Enterprise edition. On the dataset, add row-level security rules that map each manager to the region that the manager may see, and add column-level security that allows the margin column only for the finance group. Sign people in with AWS IAM Identity Center, map the groups to the Author and Reader roles, and share the dashboard with the groups. All managers then use the same dashboard and see different rows.

Question 6: Differentiate Quick Sight from Amazon Athena, Amazon Redshift, and OpenSearch Dashboards.

Maps to: Objective 6 (Analysis).

Athena runs SQL queries on data in Amazon S3, and Redshift stores and queries a data warehouse. Both produce results, and Quick Sight visualizes those results in dashboards for business users. OpenSearch Dashboards visualizes the data that is in an OpenSearch domain, mainly for search and log analytics. Quick Sight can also read from OpenSearch Service, and from many other sources.

Question 7: Design a business intelligence solution for FlashCart that serves 300 readers, reaches data in a private VPC, and controls cost.

Maps to: Objective 7 (Synthesis).

Add Quick Sight to the VPC with Enterprise edition, so it can reach the private data sources. Import the main datasets into SPICE and refresh them with the API after each ETL job, so the dashboards are fast and the data sources are spared. Use a direct query dataset only for the live page. Give a small team the Author role, and give the 300 readers the Reader role with reader capacity pricing, since they open dashboards only now and then. Add row-level security for the regions. Check the pricing page for the current costs.
