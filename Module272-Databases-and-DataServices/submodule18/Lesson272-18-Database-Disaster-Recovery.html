1. Recovery Objectives and Disaster Recovery Strategies

Problem Statement

FlashCart’s order database runs in one Region. The CTO asks what happens if that whole Region fails. The business sets these rules: orders can lose only a few minutes of data, and checkout must be back within a few minutes. The sales reports can be rebuilt, and they can wait a day.

The CFO wants to pay for fast recovery only where it matters.

Your challenge:

  • Explain the two recovery objectives.
  • Choose a strategy for the orders and a strategy for the reports.
  • Say what backups alone can and cannot do.

1.1 Two Recovery Objectives

  • Disaster recovery (DR) is the recovery of a workload after a disaster. A disaster can be larger than one data center, for example the loss of a Region.
  • The recovery point objective (RPO) is how much data you can afford to lose, measured as time.
  • The recovery time objective (RTO) is how long you can afford to be down.
  • A smaller RPO and RTO cost more, so each database gets the objectives that its business value justifies.

1.2 Four DR Strategies

StrategyRPORTOWhat runs in the DR RegionCost
Backup and restoreHoursLess than 24 hoursNothing runs. You restore the database from a copied snapshot.Low
Pilot lightTens of minutesTens of minutesThe databases are always on and synchronized. The application stack is switched off and starts on demand.Medium
Warm standbyMinutesMinutesA scaled-down copy of the whole application and database that accepts a smaller volume of traffic.High
Multi-site active/activeNear zeroZero or near zeroA full copy that serves traffic at the same scale. No failover task is needed.Higher

Source: AWS Prescriptive Guidance, Disaster recovery strategy for databases on AWS. The RPO and RTO values are for a full stack of application and database.

1.3 Backups Are the Baseline

  • All AWS database services provide backup and restore by default.
  • Backups protect against data loss and against logical errors, such as a bad delete. A restore creates a new database (lesson 272.1).
  • Backups stay in their Region unless you copy them. AWS Backup can copy recovery points to another Region or account (lesson 271.4), and Amazon Redshift snapshots can be copied to another Region (lesson 272.8).

1.4 Match the Strategy to the Data

  • Orders: a few minutes of data loss and a few minutes of downtime need a replicated database in a second Region, with a warm standby of the application.
  • Sales reports: they can be rebuilt and can wait a day, so backup and restore at low cost is enough.

Exam Trap

Remember these points:

  • A Multi-AZ deployment (lesson 272.1) protects against the loss of an Availability Zone. It does not protect against the loss of a Region.
  • Backups in the same Region are lost together with the Region. Copy them to another Region.
  • Replication copies mistakes, such as a bad delete, to the replica. Keep backups for logical errors (lesson 272.1).

Memory Hook

Cost climbs as the RPO and the RTO fall.

SAA-C03 Keyword Triggers

Trigger 1: “Lowest cost, and hours of downtime and data loss are acceptable”
→ Backup and restore

Trigger 2: “The database is always on in another Region, and the application is switched off until needed”
→ Pilot light

Trigger 3: “A scaled-down copy of the full stack runs in another Region”
→ Warm standby

Trigger 4: “Near-zero data loss and zero or near-zero downtime”
→ Multi-site active/active

1.5 Exam-Style Question

Written for this course.

Scenario: A company runs an order database in one Region. A new rule says that the company must recover within a few minutes and lose at most a few minutes of data. The company wants the lowest cost that meets the rule. Which solution meets the requirements?

  • A. Backup and restore: copy snapshots to another Region, and restore them after a disaster.
  • B. Pilot light: keep the databases synchronized in another Region, and start the application stack on demand.
  • C. Warm standby: keep a scaled-down copy of the application and the database running in another Region.
  • D. Multi-site active/active: run a full copy at the same scale in two Regions.
Show Answer and Explanation

Correct answer: C. Warm standby has an RPO and an RTO of minutes, and it costs less than multi-site active/active.

OptionResultReason
AIncorrectIts RPO is hours and its RTO is less than 24 hours, which is too slow.
BIncorrectIts RPO and RTO are tens of minutes, which is slower than the rule.
CCorrectIt meets a few minutes for both, at a lower cost than the next option.
DIncorrectIt meets the rule, but it has the highest cost. A cheaper option meets the rule too.

2. Database Disaster Recovery Options on AWS

Problem Statement

FlashCart chooses a warm standby for its orders. The orders are in Amazon Aurora, the carts are in Amazon DynamoDB, and the product pages keep sessions in Amazon ElastiCache.

The team must decide how each database reaches the second Region.

Your challenge:

  • Match each database to its cross-Region feature.
  • Explain the difference between Multi-AZ and the cross-Region options.
  • Say which options accept writes in the second Region.

2.1 Which Feature Protects Against Which Failure

FeatureProtects againstScope
Multi-AZ deployment (lesson 272.1)The failure of an instance or an Availability ZoneOne Region
Read replica in the same RegionNot a DR feature. It scales reads.One Region
Cross-Region read replicaThe loss of a RegionAnother Region. You promote it.
Global database, global cluster, or global tableThe loss of a RegionOther Regions
Backups copied to another RegionThe loss of data, or of a RegionAnother Region. A restore creates a new database.

Source: AWS Prescriptive Guidance, Choosing the right database for your RTO and RPO requirements.

2.3 Other Cross-Region Options in This Module

  • Amazon Redshift snapshots can be copied to another Region (lesson 272.8).
  • Amazon Keyspaces offers active-active multi-Region replication (lesson 272.5).
  • Amazon MSK Replicator copies data between clusters (lesson 272.13).

2.4 What Happens at Failover

  • With a cross-Region read replica or a global database, you promote the secondary to a database that accepts writes, and then point the application at it.
  • With DynamoDB global tables, every replica accepts writes, so there is no promotion step.
  • Automate the failover steps to cut the RTO.

Exam Trap

Remember these points:

  • Aurora Replicas in the same Region do not protect against the loss of a Region. Use an Aurora global database.
  • DynamoDB global tables are the answer for active-active writes in several Regions.
  • Multi-AZ is not cross-Region. It never answers a question about the loss of a Region.

Memory Hook

Same Region: Multi-AZ. Other Region: global.

SAA-C03 Keyword Triggers

Trigger 1: “An Aurora database must survive the loss of a Region with an RPO under a second”
→ Aurora Global Database

Trigger 2: “A NoSQL table must accept writes in two Regions”
→ DynamoDB global tables

Trigger 3: “An Amazon RDS for MySQL database needs a standby in another Region”
→ A cross-Region read replica, promoted at failover

Trigger 4: “A cache needs a copy in another Region”
→ ElastiCache Global Datastore

Trigger 5: “The lowest-cost DR, and hours of downtime are acceptable”
→ Copy backups or snapshots to another Region

3.4 Lesson Summary

This lesson covered the following points:

  • The RPO is how much data you can lose, and the RTO is how long you can be down.
  • The four strategies are backup and restore, pilot light, warm standby, and multi-site active/active. Cost rises as the RPO and the RTO fall.
  • All AWS database services give backup and restore. Backups protect against logical errors, and replication does not.
  • Multi-AZ protects against the loss of an Availability Zone. A cross-Region read replica, a global database, or a global table protects against the loss of a Region.
  • DynamoDB global tables are active-active.

Memory Hook

Pick the cheapest strategy that still meets the RPO and the RTO.

4. Knowledge Check

Answer each question in your own words before you open the model answer. The questions were written for this course.

Question 1: Explain disaster recovery, the RPO, and the RTO.

Maps to: Objective 1 (Comprehension).

Disaster recovery is the recovery of a workload after a disaster, such as the loss of a Region. The RPO is how much data you can afford to lose, measured as time. The RTO is how long you can afford to be down. Both set the cost of the strategy that you need.

Question 2: Describe the four DR strategies in order of cost.

Maps to: Objective 2 (Comprehension).

Backup and restore costs least, with an RPO of hours and an RTO of less than 24 hours. Pilot light keeps the databases synchronized and the application stack off, with tens of minutes for both. Warm standby runs a scaled-down copy, with minutes for both. Multi-site active/active runs a full copy, with near-zero data loss and zero or near-zero downtime, and it costs the most.

Question 3: Differentiate Multi-AZ, read replicas, and copies in another Region by the failure that each protects against.

Maps to: Objective 3 (Comprehension).

Multi-AZ protects against the failure of an instance or an Availability Zone inside one Region. A read replica in the same Region scales reads. A cross-Region read replica, a global database, a global table, or a backup copied to another Region protects against the loss of a Region.

Question 4: Match Aurora, DynamoDB, and ElastiCache to their cross-Region features, and compare their RTO and RPO.

Maps to: Objective 4 (Application).

Aurora uses a global database, with a typical RTO under 1 minute and a typical RPO under 1 second. DynamoDB uses global tables, which are active-active, with a zero or near-zero RTO and a sub-second RPO. ElastiCache uses Global Datastore, with an RTO of usually minutes and an RPO in seconds.

Question 5: The orders can lose a few minutes of data and must be back in a few minutes, and the reports can wait a day. Select a strategy for each.

Maps to: Objective 5 (Application).

Use warm standby for the orders, with a replicated database and a scaled-down application in a second Region. Use backup and restore for the reports, because they can be rebuilt and can wait, and it costs the least.

Question 6: Design a DR plan for an Aurora order database, a DynamoDB cart table, and a Redshift report warehouse.

Maps to: Objective 6 (Synthesis).

Give each tier its own strategy. For the Aurora orders, use a global database with a warm standby of the application. For the DynamoDB carts, use global tables, which accept writes in every Region. For the Redshift warehouse, copy the snapshots to another Region and restore them if the Region is lost. Keep backups of all three for logical errors, and automate the failover steps.
