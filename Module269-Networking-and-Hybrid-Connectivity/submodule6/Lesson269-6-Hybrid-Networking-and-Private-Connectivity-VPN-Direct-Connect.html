1. What Is Hybrid Connectivity?

Problem Statement

Your company’s data center is getting older. The team wants to add database capacity and move some applications to AWS, but keep part of the infrastructure on premises. Servers in both places must talk to each other using private IP addresses, as if they were on one network, without exposing them to the internet.

Before you can choose a service, you need to understand what kind of connection this is, and why companies build it.

1.1 What Is Hybrid Connectivity?

  • Extends your on-premises network into an AWS VPC.
  • Connects infrastructure across environments, including between two cloud hyperscalers (for example, AWS and Azure, or AWS and Google Cloud).
  • Creates a unified network in which resources on both sides communicate privately.
  • It is called hybrid because part of the workload stays outside AWS while the rest runs inside your VPC.

1.2 Why Do Organizations Need Hybrid Connectivity?

  • Expand capacity. You may need more capacity, or more room for your databases, than your data center can provide.
  • Modernize in stages. Your infrastructure is getting older, and you want to keep only part of it on premises while the rest moves to AWS.
  • Extend another cloud into AWS. You already run workloads on another hyperscaler, such as Google Cloud or Azure, and want to expand into AWS as well.

In every case, the goal is the same: a network where the resources on both sides can reach each other privately.

1.3 How Do You Connect?

AWS gives you two ways to build the connection:

  • AWS Site-to-Site VPN — encrypted tunnels between your network and AWS. This is the focus of tabs 2 to 5.
  • AWS Direct Connect — a dedicated network connection from your location to AWS. This is the focus of tab 6.

We start with the VPN family in the next tab, and cover Direct Connect after the VPN deep dive.

2. AWS VPN at a Glance

Problem Statement

Your company runs its new applications in a VPC, but its customer database and most of its staff are still in an office data center. Servers in the VPC must talk to servers in the office using private IP addresses, and none of that traffic may travel unencrypted across the internet.

Buying new physical lines would take weeks. You need an encrypted connection that can be set up in hours. A few engineers who work from home also need secure access to the same private servers, without exposing those servers to the internet.

2.1 Overview of AWS VPN

  • AWS VPN is a managed service that creates encrypted connections between resources in your VPC and networks or devices outside AWS.
  • The traffic travels over the public internet, but it is wrapped in an encrypted tunnel, so anyone who captures it cannot read it.
  • AWS manages the VPN endpoints on its side. You do not run or patch any VPN servers in AWS.
  • The family has two services: Site-to-Site VPN (network to network) and Client VPN (single user to network).

3. Architecture 1: Site-to-Site VPN With a Virtual Private Gateway

Problem Statement

An application server in a private subnet of your VPC must query an enterprise database that still lives in the company data center. The security team has approved an IPsec VPN. The network team now asks for the exact picture: which addresses, which route table entry, which gateway, and which tunnel the traffic uses, and which parts AWS manages and which parts they must configure.

3.1 Core Components: Four Building Blocks

To read any Site-to-Site VPN design, you need four building blocks. Two of them are on the AWS side, one is an AWS resource that describes your device, and one is the device itself.

  1. Virtual private gateway (VGW). The logical VPN endpoint, or concentrator, on the AWS side, attached to a single VPC. It accepts the VPN connection from your customer gateway device, and it is the target in the VPC route tables for traffic going to your on-premises network.
  2. Transit gateway (TGW). The alternative AWS-side endpoint, used when you connect several VPCs and your on-premises network. It works as a centralized virtual router, so a VPN attached to it gives transitive routing across the attached VPCs, with no need for a separate virtual private gateway per VPC (tab 4).
  3. Customer gateway (an AWS resource). A resource that represents your on-premises VPN device inside AWS. It stores information about the device: its public IP address, its BGP ASN for dynamic routing, and optional certificate details (a private certificate from AWS Private CA). It does not create or configure the device.
  4. Customer gateway device (on premises). The actual router, firewall, or software appliance in your data center, for example a Cisco router, a Palo Alto firewall, a Fortinet FortiGate, or strongSwan software. You configure it to bring up the IPsec tunnels with AWS (section 3.4).

A VPN connection joins the AWS-side gateway (item 1 or 2) to the customer gateway (item 3) with two redundant IPsec tunnels, which end on your device (item 4). The two tunnels end in different Availability Zones. If one has maintenance or loses connectivity, traffic fails over to the other (sections 3.3 and 5.3).

Memory Hook

Customer gateway = your side (the AWS resource that describes your device). Virtual private gateway = the AWS side. The VPN connection joins them with two tunnels.

Exam Trap

The customer gateway is not the device.

The customer gateway in the AWS console is only a record of your device’s public IP (and ASN). It lives in AWS. The customer gateway device is the real router or firewall on your premises, and you must configure it. AWS gives you a downloadable configuration file to help.

3.2 The Baseline Topology

This is the simplest complete design: one on-premises network, one VPC, and a virtual private gateway (VGW) as the AWS-side endpoint. The addresses are examples.

3.3 Following a Packet Through the Topology

Following a Packet Through the Topology.
StepWhat happens
1The application server in the private subnet (10.0.1.0/24) sends a packet to the database in the corporate network (192.168.0.0/16).
2The subnet’s route table is checked. The destination does not match 10.0.0.0/16 (local), but it matches 192.168.0.0/16, whose target is the virtual private gateway vgw-xxxx.
3The VGW wraps the packet in IPsec and encrypts it, then sends it through a tunnel to the public IP address of your customer gateway device (203.0.113.12).
4The encrypted packet crosses the public internet. Anyone who captures it sees only encrypted data between the two tunnel endpoints.
5The customer gateway device decrypts the packet and forwards it into the corporate network, to the database. The reply travels back the same way, so your device must route the VPC range (10.0.0.0/16) into the tunnel.
  • The route table entry is what steers traffic into the VPN. You can add the 192.168.0.0/16 route by hand, or turn on route propagation so the VGW adds it for you (lesson 269.1 covers route tables).
  • Two tunnels, two endpoints. Each VPN connection has two tunnels, each with its own public IP address on the AWS side, and each terminating in a different Availability Zone. From AWS toward your network, one tunnel is preferred and the other is the standby; if the preferred one fails, traffic moves to the other. From your device toward AWS, either tunnel can carry traffic.
  • Always configure both tunnels on your device. AWS maintenance can briefly disable one of them.

3.4 Customer Gateway Devices in Detail

  • A customer gateway device is a physical or software appliance that you own or manage on your side of the connection. You or your network administrator must configure it to work with the VPN connection.
  • The customer gateway resource you create in AWS only tells AWS about the device, for example its public-facing IP address. It does not create or configure the device.
  • AWS supports a number of device types and provides downloadable configuration files for them when you create the connection. You can also find software VPN appliances in the AWS Marketplace.
  • If a device failure happens within AWS, the connection automatically fails over to the second tunnel. AWS also performs routine maintenance, and tunnel endpoints can be replaced, which may briefly disable one tunnel. That is why the device must be configured for both tunnels.

3.4.1 What the Device Must Set Up for Each Tunnel

Each tunnel contains an IKE security association, an IPsec security association, and, for dynamic routing, a BGP peering. You configure these on the device for each of the two tunnels.

What the Device Must Set Up for Each Tunnel.
PartWhat it isNotes
1. IKE security associationSet up first between the virtual private gateway and your device, authenticated with a pre-shared key or a private certificate from AWS Private Certificate Authority.By default your device starts the negotiation; you can configure AWS to start it instead.
2. IPsec security associationKeys negotiated through IKE form the IPsec security association, which encrypts the traffic in the tunnel.Use dead peer detection so the device notices when the other end stops responding.
3. Tunnel interfaceA logical interface on the device that receives the traffic for the tunnel. It holds the IP address used for the BGP peering.Site-to-Site VPN is route-based: your device sends traffic into the tunnel because of a route, so route the VPC range to this interface.
4. BGP peering (optional)Used with dynamic routing to exchange routes between your device and the virtual private gateway.All BGP traffic is encrypted and sent inside the IPsec security association. Not needed with static routing.

3.4.2 Practical Requirements to Remember

  • A static public IP address for the device’s outside interface, which is the endpoint of the IPsec tunnels. If the device sits behind a NAT device, use the NAT device’s public IP address in the customer gateway, and turn on NAT traversal.
  • Firewall rules: if a firewall sits between the device and the internet, it must allow IKE on UDP 500, NAT traversal on UDP 4500 when used, and the IPsec (ESP) traffic itself.
  • IPsec and, for BGP, BGP on the same device (section 5.1.2).
  • Both tunnels configured, so that maintenance on one does not interrupt the connection.
  • Check the supported devices and the downloadable configuration file for your vendor before you buy or choose a device.

3.5 AWS-Managed VPN and Redundant Connections

AWS-managed VPN. Amazon VPC lets you create an IPsec VPN connection between your remote network and your VPC over the internet. Consider this approach when you want an AWS-managed VPN endpoint with automated redundancy and failover built into the AWS side of the connection. You do not build, patch, or scale VPN servers in AWS.

Redundant connections. The virtual private gateway also supports, and encourages, several customer gateway connections. This lets you build redundancy and failover on your side of the connection as well, by using more than one customer gateway device.

Exam Trap

Tunnels UP, but no traffic.

If the tunnels are up but instances cannot reach on-premises servers, the cause is almost always one of these: a missing route in the VPC route table (or route propagation not enabled), a security group or network ACL that does not allow the on-premises CIDR, or the on-premises side not routing the VPC CIDR into the tunnel.

4. Architecture 2: Site-to-Site VPN With a Transit Gateway

Problem Statement

The company now has three VPCs: Production, Staging, and Shared Services. All of them must reach the data center, and some of them must reach each other. Building a separate VPN for every VPC would mean three sets of tunnels, three gateways, and three route configurations to maintain. You want one place where the VPN ends and where routing between all the networks is controlled.

4.1 The Multi-VPC Topology

In this hub-and-spoke design, a transit gateway (TGW) replaces the virtual private gateway as the AWS-side endpoint. The VPN connection attaches to the transit gateway, and so does every VPC (transit gateways are covered in lesson 269.3). The addresses are examples.

Redundant connections. A transit gateway also supports, and encourages, several customer gateway connections. As with a virtual private gateway, this lets you build redundancy and failover on your side of the VPN connection, by using more than one customer gateway. Each VPN connection appears as its own VPN attachment on the transit gateway.

4.2 How the Design Works

  • One VPN, many VPCs. The VPN connection is an attachment of the transit gateway. Each VPC is also an attachment, so all three VPCs share the same tunnels to the data center.
  • Centralized routing. The transit gateway has its own route tables. They decide which attachment can reach which, which makes transitive routing possible: VPC to VPC, and VPC to data center, through the hub. You can keep Staging away from Production by controlling these route tables.
  • Dynamic routing. The customer gateway device (in this example a Cisco, Palo Alto, or Fortinet appliance with ASN 65000) advertises 172.16.0.0/12 to the transit gateway over BGP, so the VPCs learn the data center routes automatically (section 5.1).
  • Two tunnels carry the traffic, just as in the virtual private gateway design.

AWS announced 5 Gbps tunnels in November 2025. Older study material says that a VPN tunnel is limited to 1.25 Gbps and that ECMP is the only way to go higher. Both statements are now incomplete: a transit gateway can use a large bandwidth tunnel or ECMP. For exam questions, a transit gateway is the answer to "more bandwidth" either way.

5. Deep Dive: Technical Specifications & Security Parameters

Problem Statement

The architecture is approved, and now the security and network teams want the details. How are routes exchanged, and what ASN do we need? Which encryption and integrity algorithms protect the tunnels? How are keys rotated? What happens when a tunnel or our VPN device fails? How much traffic can it carry, and how do we watch it?

5.1 Routing Options: Static vs. Dynamic (BGP)

Routing Options: Static vs. Dynamic (BGP).
AspectDynamic routing (BGP) — recommendedStatic routing
How routes are learnedYour device advertises its prefixes to AWS over BGP, and AWS advertises the VPC routes back.You type the on-premises prefixes into the VPN connection.
When to use itYour device supports BGP. AWS recommends this option.Your device does not support BGP.

FailoverBGP liveness detection helps traffic move to the second tunnel when the first fails.Relies on health checks that your device may perform, and on dead peer detection (section 5.3).

  • If you choose BGP, you cannot also add static routes to that connection. If a virtual private gateway holds the same route as both a static route and a BGP route, the static route is preferred.
  • Routing always uses the most specific route (longest prefix match), so advertising specific prefixes is the way to steer traffic.
  • For the VPC to use the routes, the route table needs an entry pointing to the gateway, added by hand or through route propagation.

5.1.1 BGP ASNs

  • BGP identifies each network by an Autonomous System Number (ASN). The customer gateway holds your ASN; the AWS side has its own.
  • The Amazon-side ASN of a virtual private gateway or transit gateway must be a private ASN: 64512 to 65534 for a 16-bit ASN, or 4200000000 to 4294967294 for a 32-bit ASN. The default is 64512, and a virtual private gateway’s ASN cannot be changed after you create it.
  • Your own ASN (in the customer gateway) is your network’s ASN, public or private. In the example in Image 4a the device uses the private ASN 65000.
  • With VPN CloudHub, every site needs a unique ASN (section 5.6).

Watch the Wording: Those Ranges Belong to the Amazon Side

Some summaries describe the ranges 64512–65534 and 4200000000–4294967294 as the range for the on-premises ASN. AWS documents them as the allowed private ASN range for the Amazon side of the BGP session.

5.1.2 What BGP Lets You Influence

  • Dynamic routing uses BGP peering to exchange routing information between AWS and your customer gateway device.
  • In your BGP advertisements you can set routing priorities, policies, and weights (metrics). These influence the network path between your network and AWS, for example to make one connection preferred and another a backup.
  • Both sessions must end on the same device. The IPsec session and the BGP session must be terminated on the same customer gateway device, so that device must be able to handle both.

Exam Trap

The same device must end IPsec and BGP.

If a design uses dynamic routing, the customer gateway device must be able to terminate both the IPsec tunnels and the BGP sessions. A device that supports only IPsec points you to static routing. (Source: AWS whitepaper "Amazon Virtual Private Cloud Connectivity Options", section AWS Site-to-Site VPN.)

5.3 Failover and High Availability

  • Two tunnels, two Availability Zones. Each tunnel of a connection ends in a different Availability Zone. If one becomes unavailable, for example during maintenance, traffic is automatically routed over the other.
  • Direction matters. Traffic from your network to AWS can use both tunnels. Traffic from AWS to your network prefers one tunnel and fails over to the other if there is a failure on the AWS side.
  • How a failure is detected. With BGP, liveness detection helps traffic move to the second tunnel. Dead peer detection notices a dead peer for both routing types. Devices without BGP may also run health checks.
  • No fixed failover time. The sources used for this lesson do not promise a specific failover time, so test your own design instead of assuming one.
  • No ECMP on a virtual private gateway. The second tunnel is for failover, not for extra bandwidth. To add up tunnels you need a transit gateway.
  • Your device is a single point of failure unless you add a second customer gateway device and a second VPN connection (Image 3e).

Exam Trap

Two tunnels do not mean double bandwidth.

On a virtual private gateway, the second tunnel is a backup. To add up the bandwidth of several tunnels you need a transit gateway with ECMP (or a large bandwidth tunnel).

6. AWS Direct Connect (DX)

Problem Statement

The company’s data center sends about 20 TB of backups to AWS every night, and its trading application needs predictable low latency to servers in a VPC. Over the VPN, throughput and latency change with internet conditions, and each tunnel is limited in bandwidth. The compliance team also prefers that regulated data does not cross the public internet at all.

The business can wait several weeks for the new link. You need consistent, high-bandwidth, private connectivity, and you need to understand its parts, its options, its security, and how to make it resilient.

6.1 What Is AWS Direct Connect?

  • AWS Direct Connect (DX) establishes a dedicated, private network connection between your data center, office, or colocation environment and AWS.
  • The traffic does not travel over the public internet. It uses a physical link that ends at an AWS Direct Connect location, and from there it enters the AWS network.
  • You then create virtual interfaces on that link to reach your VPCs (private and transit interfaces) or AWS public services such as Amazon S3 (public interface).
  • It is the second AWS option for hybrid connectivity, next to Site-to-Site VPN (tabs 3 to 5). Both give you one network across your premises and AWS, but they differ in how the link is built.
  • It is not encrypted by default. The path is private, but you add encryption yourself when you need it (section 6.7).

6.4 Virtual Interfaces

A virtual interface (VIF) is a logical connection over your Direct Connect link. It has a VLAN (802.1Q tag) and a BGP session, and its type decides what you can reach. The type is chosen when you create it.

6.4.1 Virtual Interfaces (VIF) Types

Concept 1 of 4

Private VIF

  • Reaches: resources in a VPC with private IP addresses.
  • Connects to: a virtual private gateway in the same Region, or a Direct Connect gateway to reach VPCs in other Regions and accounts.
  • MTU: 1500 (default) or 9001 (jumbo frames).
  • Typical use: large transfers and low-latency traffic to a VPC.

Common uses

  • Accessing private EC2 instances
  • Connecting on-premises applications to private databases
  • Extending internal networks into AWS
  • Supporting hybrid application architectures

Concept 2 of 4

Public VIF

  • Reaches: AWS public services with public IP addresses, such as Amazon S3 and DynamoDB, and public EC2 addresses. It does not give access to the private addresses of a VPC.
  • Connects to: the AWS public network, in any AWS Region (except China).
  • Typical use: reach S3 or other public endpoints over the private link, not the internet.

Possible destinations

  • Amazon S3 public endpoints
  • AWS public API endpoints
  • Publicly addressable AWS resources

Concept 3 of 4

Transit VIF

  • Reaches: a transit gateway, and through it many VPCs (and VPNs attached to it).
  • Connects to: a Direct Connect gateway that is associated with a transit gateway (section 6.5).
  • MTU: 1500 (default) or 8500 (jumbo frames).
  • Typical use: many VPCs through one interface.

Supports centralized connectivity for

  • Multiple VPCs
  • Multiple AWS accounts
  • Multiple transit gateways
  • Multi-Region architectures
  • Large enterprise networks

Concept 4 of 4

Virtual Interfaces: The Basics

Pick the type by what you want to reach:

  • Private VIF: a VPC
  • Public VIF: AWS public services
  • Transit VIF: a transit gateway, through a Direct Connect gateway

What every VIF needs, and good to know

  • Needs: a VLAN ID, your BGP ASN (public or private), the IP addresses for the BGP peering, and a BGP MD5 authentication key (AWS can generate one). IPv4 and IPv6 BGP sessions are both supported.
  • One physical connection, many VIFs. You can run a private VIF for your VPC and a public VIF for S3 on the same connection, each on its own VLAN.
  • Jumbo frames must be supported by the connection as well. Changing the MTU of a VIF can briefly update the underlying connection.
  • BFD (Bidirectional Forwarding Detection) is enabled automatically on Direct Connect virtual interfaces, but it takes effect only after you configure it on your router. It helps detect a failed link faster than BGP timers alone.
Exam trap: pick the VIF by what you want to reach

Use a private VIF to access a VPC, a public VIF to privately access AWS public services such as S3 or DynamoDB (over the Direct Connect link, not the internet), and a transit VIF to connect to a transit gateway (through a Direct Connect gateway). A private VIF does not give you access to public AWS services.

6.5 Direct Connect Gateway

A private VIF can reach one VPC through its virtual private gateway. A Direct Connect gateway (DXGW) removes that limit. It is a globally available resource that connects your Direct Connect link to many VPCs, in any Region and any account.

  • Many Regions need a Direct Connect gateway. If you want to set up Direct Connect to one or more VPCs in many different Regions (same account), you must use a Direct Connect gateway. A private VIF that connects straight to a virtual private gateway reaches only the Region of that gateway.
  • With a private VIF: you associate the DX gateway with the virtual private gateways of the VPCs you want to reach, in the same or different Regions and accounts. Each association proposal is accepted by the DX gateway’s owner.
  • With a transit VIF: you associate the DX gateway with a transit gateway, and reach all the VPCs and VPNs attached to it. A DX gateway can also be associated with an AWS Cloud WAN core network.
  • Allowed prefixes: when you associate a gateway, you give a list of prefixes that the DX gateway advertises to your on-premises network. For a virtual private gateway, the allowed prefixes must be the same as, or wider than, the VPC CIDR. A VPC with 10.0.0.0/16 can use 10.0.0.0/16 or 10.0.0.0/15.
  • ASNs must differ. If you associate a transit gateway with a DX gateway, they must use different ASNs. Using the default 64512 for both makes the association fail. Use unique ASNs for transit gateways in different Regions as well.
  • A DX gateway does not route between your VPCs. Traffic between the associated VPCs is not forwarded by the gateway. It forwards traffic between your on-premises network and each VPC. Use a transit gateway or VPC peering for VPC-to-VPC traffic.
  • A DX gateway that is already associated with a virtual private gateway, or attached to a private VIF, cannot be associated with a transit gateway. Use a separate DX gateway for the transit VIF.
  • There are no extra charges to use a multi-account Direct Connect gateway. Data transfer out for private and transit VIFs is billed to the account that is responsible for the data transfer.

6.6 Routing Over Direct Connect

  • BGP is required. Each virtual interface has its own BGP session. You advertise your on-premises prefixes, and AWS advertises the VPC prefixes (or the allowed prefixes of the DX gateway).
  • VPC route tables need a route to your on-premises range through the gateway, added by hand or by route propagation, just as with a VPN (lesson 269.1 covers route tables).
  • Same prefix, two paths: Direct Connect wins. For the same prefix, a virtual private gateway prefers BGP routes from Direct Connect over static VPN routes, and static VPN routes over BGP routes from a VPN. For a transit gateway, a Direct Connect gateway route is also preferred over a VPN route. Longest prefix match still applies first.
  • Influencing the path: you can use standard BGP attributes on your router, and Direct Connect also offers local preference communities to choose among several Direct Connect paths.

6.8.1 Site-to-Site VPN as a Backup for Direct Connect

A common and lower-cost design keeps Direct Connect as the primary path and adds a Site-to-Site VPN over the internet as the backup.

  • Advertise the same on-premises prefixes over both paths. AWS prefers the Direct Connect route when the prefixes are the same, and uses the VPN when the Direct Connect routes disappear.
  • A VPN adds no second physical path and has lower bandwidth, so plan for reduced capacity while the backup is active.
  • A backup VPN does not appear on its own. You must configure it, set the BGP preferences on your router so that your traffic also prefers Direct Connect, and test the failover. If AWS prefers one path and your router prefers the other, traffic can become asymmetric.
  • With a transit gateway, attach the VPN to the same transit gateway and propagate both attachments into the route tables.

7. Transit Gateway: Site-to-Site VPN ECMP

Problem Statement

The company’s network has grown. Many VPCs are peered together, several VPN connections reach different VPCs, and a Direct Connect gateway reaches others. Every new VPC or site means more links and more routes to build and maintain, and the network diagram is hard to read.

You want one hub that connects the VPCs, the VPN, and Direct Connect, and that also lets you add up the bandwidth of several VPN tunnels.

7.1 Network Topologies Can Become Complicated

A common network topology in AWS quickly becomes complicated. You may have many VPCs that you peer together, VPN connections from your customer gateway to several of them, and Direct Connect with a Direct Connect gateway to reach multiple VPCs at a time. Every new connection adds more links to build, route, and maintain.

AWS created the transit gateway to solve this problem (see lesson 269.3). It gives you transitive connectivity in a hub-and-spoke (star) design between thousands of VPCs, your on-premises data center, Site-to-Site VPN, and Direct Connect.

  • Many VPCs, one hub. You attach the VPCs to the transit gateway, so you do not need to peer them with each other. They are connected transitively through the hub, so all the attached VPCs can talk to each other. The transit gateway route tables can limit this when you need to.
  • Direct Connect. You connect a Direct Connect gateway to the transit gateway, through a transit VIF, so that one Direct Connect connection gives you access to many VPCs (sections 6.4 and 6.5).
  • Site-to-Site VPN. You connect your customer gateway and its VPN connection to the transit gateway, which again gives you access to all the VPCs attached to it (tab 4).

7.2 Transit Gateway: Site-to-Site VPN ECMP

  • ECMP = Equal-cost multi-path routing.
  • A routing strategy that allows a packet to be forwarded over multiple best paths.
  • Use case: create multiple Site-to-Site VPN connections to increase the bandwidth of your connection to AWS.
  • Why it helps: one VPN tunnel carries up to 1.25 Gbps. Without ECMP, a virtual private gateway uses one tunnel at a time. With ECMP on a transit gateway, the tunnels of several VPN connections share the traffic, so the total bandwidth adds up. For example, two VPN connections give four tunnels, or about 5 Gbps in total at best.
  • What you need: a transit gateway (a virtual private gateway does not support ECMP) and dynamic routing with BGP, so the transit gateway learns equal-cost routes over each tunnel.
  • Good to know: traffic is spread across the paths per flow, so a single flow still uses one tunnel. Each extra VPN connection adds its own connection-hour fee.
  • Another option: a large bandwidth tunnel gives up to 5 Gbps in one tunnel (section 4.4).

Exam Trap

ECMP needs a transit gateway.

If a question asks for more than one tunnel’s bandwidth over a Site-to-Site VPN, the answer is a transit gateway with ECMP and several VPN connections. A virtual private gateway cannot do this.

9. Knowledge Check

No multiple choice. Answer each question in your own words first, then expand the model answer to compare.

Question 1. What is hybrid connectivity, why do organizations need it, and what are the two AWS options for building it?

Show the model answer

Hybrid connectivity extends your on-premises network into an AWS VPC, creating one unified network in which resources on both sides communicate privately. Organizations need it to expand capacity or databases, to keep only part of an aging infrastructure on premises, or to extend another cloud provider’s workloads into AWS. AWS offers two options: Site-to-Site VPN, which uses encrypted tunnels, and AWS Direct Connect, which is a dedicated network connection.

Question 2. What is AWS VPN, and how do Site-to-Site VPN and Client VPN differ?

Show the model answer

AWS VPN is a managed service that creates encrypted connections between your VPC and the outside, over the public internet. Site-to-Site VPN connects a whole network, such as a data center or branch office, to AWS through IPsec tunnels, and needs a customer gateway device on your side. Client VPN connects individual users’ devices through an OpenVPN-based TLS tunnel to a Client VPN endpoint, and authenticates users with Active Directory, certificates, or SAML single sign-on.

Question 3. An application server at 10.0.1.0/24 sends a packet to a database at 192.168.0.0/16 over a Site-to-Site VPN with a virtual private gateway. Trace the packet.

Show the model answer

The packet leaves the server, and the subnet’s route table matches 192.168.0.0/16, whose target is the virtual private gateway. The VGW encrypts it with IPsec and sends it through a tunnel, over the internet, to the public IP of the customer gateway device. The device decrypts it and forwards it into the corporate network to the database. The reply returns the same way, so the device must route the VPC range into the tunnel.

Question 4. Name the components of a Site-to-Site VPN connection and say who manages what, including redundancy.

Show the model answer

The customer gateway device is the appliance in your network, and you configure and patch it. The customer gateway is the AWS resource that records the device’s public IP and, for BGP, its ASN. The virtual private gateway (or a transit gateway) is the AWS-side endpoint, managed by AWS with automated redundancy and failover on its side. The VPN connection joins the two ends with two tunnels. Redundancy on your side, such as a second device, a second VPN connection, and the routing and failover between your devices, is your responsibility.

Question 5. What must a customer gateway device set up for each tunnel, and what practical requirements apply to it?

Show the model answer

For each tunnel it needs an IKE security association (authenticated with a pre-shared key or a private certificate), an IPsec security association formed from the negotiated keys, a tunnel interface that receives the VPC-bound traffic (the service is route-based), and, for dynamic routing, a BGP peering whose traffic is encrypted inside the IPsec security association. It needs a static public IP address, which is the NAT device’s public IP if it sits behind NAT, firewall rules for UDP 500, UDP 4500 when NAT traversal is used, and IPsec traffic, and it must be configured for both tunnels. AWS provides a downloadable configuration file, but you configure the device yourself.

Question 6. A company has three VPCs that must all reach the data center. Why use a transit gateway instead of a virtual private gateway?

Show the model answer

A virtual private gateway attaches to one VPC, so you would need a VPN per VPC. A transit gateway lets one VPN connection serve every attached VPC and routes between them using its own route tables, which also lets you control which VPCs can reach each other. It also unlocks ECMP, Accelerated VPN, IPv6 inside the tunnels, and 5 Gbps tunnels, at the cost of attachment and data processing fees.

Question 7. When would you choose static routing instead of dynamic routing, what ASN rules apply, and what must be true of the device if you use BGP?

Show the model answer

Choose static routing when your customer gateway device does not support BGP; you then list the on-premises prefixes yourself. With BGP, the device advertises its prefixes automatically and BGP liveness detection helps failover. The Amazon-side ASN must be a private ASN, 64512 to 65534 or 4200000000 to 4294967294, and your own ASN is recorded in the customer gateway. With BGP, the IPsec and BGP sessions must end on the same customer gateway device, so the device must support both. In BGP advertisements you can set priorities, policies, and weights to influence the path.

Question 8. Describe the main security parameters of a Site-to-Site VPN tunnel.

Show the model answer

Each tunnel is an IPsec tunnel negotiated with IKEv1 or IKEv2. You can choose encryption (AES128, AES256, or their GCM variants) and integrity (SHA1 or SHA2 variants), and the keys are rotated at the phase 1 and phase 2 lifetimes (defaults of 8 hours and 1 hour). Dead peer detection notices a dead peer, with a default timeout of 40 seconds. Authentication uses a pre-shared key by default, or certificates from AWS Private CA. Ports are UDP 500, plus UDP 4500 with NAT traversal.

Question 9. Why does every VPN connection have two tunnels, and how does failover work?

Show the model answer

The two tunnels give redundancy: each ends in a different Availability Zone, and AWS maintenance can briefly disable one. You must configure your device to use both. Traffic from your network to AWS can use both tunnels, while traffic from AWS to your network prefers one and fails over to the other. BGP liveness detection and dead peer detection help notice failures. On a virtual private gateway there is no ECMP, so the second tunnel adds availability, not bandwidth.

Question 10. A company needs about 4 Gbps and high availability from one data center to 10 VPCs over the internet. Design the VPN solution.

Show the model answer

Attach all 10 VPCs to a transit gateway and create Site-to-Site VPN connections to it with BGP. To reach 4 Gbps, either use large bandwidth tunnels (up to 5 Gbps each) or create several connections and enable ECMP to combine their tunnels. For availability, use two customer gateway devices, each with its own VPN connection and both tunnels configured, and alarm on the TunnelState metric in CloudWatch.

Question 11. A company has three branch offices with separate internet connections. How can they communicate with each other through AWS, and what are the requirements?

Show the model answer

Use VPN CloudHub. Create one virtual private gateway and one customer gateway per site, each with a unique BGP ASN, and a dynamically routed VPN connection from each site. Each site advertises its own prefix, and the gateway re-advertises the routes so every site can reach the others. The sites’ IP ranges must not overlap.

Question 12. Both tunnels show UP, but instances cannot reach the on-premises servers. What do you check?

Show the model answer

Check that the VPC route table has a route for the on-premises CIDR through the gateway (or that route propagation is on), that security groups and network ACLs allow the on-premises CIDR, and that the on-premises side routes the VPC CIDR into the tunnel.

Question 13. What is AWS Direct Connect, and how does it differ from Site-to-Site VPN?

Show the model answer

Direct Connect is a dedicated, private network connection between your premises and AWS. It ends at a Direct Connect location, connected to your router by a cross-connect, and it does not use the public internet. Compared with Site-to-Site VPN, it gives consistent latency and much higher bandwidth, and it has a lower data transfer out rate, but it takes weeks to provision, costs port hours, and is not encrypted by default. A VPN is encrypted by default, starts in hours, and uses the internet.

Question 14. Compare dedicated and hosted Direct Connect connections.

Show the model answer

A dedicated connection is a physical port of 1, 10, 100, or 400 Gbps that is yours alone, ordered in the Direct Connect console, and it supports many virtual interfaces (up to 50 private or public and 4 transit). A hosted connection is provisioned by a Direct Connect Partner from the partner’s capacity, with speeds from 50 Mbps to 25 Gbps, and it supports a single virtual interface. Dedicated suits high bandwidth and MACsec (Media Access Control Security); hosted suits smaller or more flexible needs.

Question 15. Name the three types of virtual interface and what each reaches. What does a Direct Connect gateway add?

Show the model answer

A private VIF reaches resources in a VPC through a virtual private gateway, a public VIF reaches AWS public services such as S3 and DynamoDB, and a transit VIF reaches a transit gateway and the VPCs attached to it. A Direct Connect gateway is a global resource that lets one private VIF reach virtual private gateways of VPCs in any Region and account, or one transit VIF reach a transit gateway. It does not route traffic between the VPCs. Its allowed prefixes control what it advertises to your network, and its ASN must differ from the transit gateway’s ASN.

Question 16. Is traffic over Direct Connect encrypted? How can you encrypt it?

Show the model answer

No. Direct Connect is private but not encrypted by default. You can use MACsec, a Layer 2 option for dedicated connections of 10, 100, or 400 Gbps at selected locations, which protects the link between your device and the AWS device. You can also run an IPsec Site-to-Site VPN over Direct Connect, for example a private IP VPN over a transit VIF, or encrypt at the application layer with TLS.

Question 17. A company needs a 99.99% SLA for its Direct Connect connectivity and a low-cost fallback. Design it.

Show the model answer

Use the maximum resiliency model: dedicated connections on separate AWS devices in at least two Direct Connect locations, typically two connections in each. Use BFD on the virtual interfaces and test failover with the Resiliency Toolkit. For a low-cost fallback, add a Site-to-Site VPN over the internet and advertise the same prefixes over both paths. AWS prefers the Direct Connect route when the prefixes are the same, and uses the VPN if the Direct Connect routes disappear. Configure your router so its own preference matches, and test it.

Question 18. Why does a physical Direct Connect connection still require a virtual interface?

Show the model answer

The physical connection only brings your link to AWS. It carries no traffic until you create a virtual interface on it. The virtual interface is the logical part, with its own VLAN and BGP session, and its type decides what the connection can reach: a private VIF reaches a VPC, a public VIF reaches AWS public services such as S3 and DynamoDB, and a transit VIF reaches a transit gateway through a Direct Connect gateway. One physical connection can carry several virtual interfaces, each on its own VLAN.

Question 19. Why is Direct Connect not considered encrypted by default?

Show the model answer

Direct Connect gives you a private, dedicated path that avoids the public internet, but private does not mean encrypted. Nothing on the link encrypts the traffic unless you add it. The options are MACsec, which encrypts at Layer 2 on dedicated connections of 10, 100, or 400 Gbps at selected locations, an IPsec VPN over Direct Connect (for example a private IP VPN over a transit VIF), or encryption in the application, such as TLS. A Site-to-Site VPN, in contrast, is always IPsec-encrypted.

Question 20. What is the difference between a virtual private gateway and a Transit Gateway?

Show the model answer

A virtual private gateway attaches to a single VPC and is the simple, low-cost AWS-side endpoint for a VPN or a private virtual interface. A transit gateway is a central hub that connects many VPCs and your on-premises network, with its own route tables and transitive routing. It also supports ECMP, Accelerated VPN, and large bandwidth tunnels for VPNs, but adds attachment and data processing fees. With Direct Connect, a private VIF reaches a virtual private gateway (directly in the same Region, or through a Direct Connect gateway for other Regions), and a transit VIF reaches a transit gateway through a Direct Connect gateway.

Question 21. Which monitoring information would help distinguish a physical connection failure from a BGP failure?

Show the model answer

Start with the physical layer. The connection state shows whether the connection itself is up, and the light level and error metrics (such as CRC errors) for the port point to a fiber, optic, or cross-connect problem. If the connection is up and healthy, look at the virtual interface: its BGP peer status, whether routes are being received and advertised, and its traffic metrics. A connection that is up with BGP down usually points to configuration, such as the VLAN, ASN, peering IP addresses, or BGP key, or to the router. A connection that is down, or with poor light levels, points to the physical path. BFD helps detect a failed link quickly.
